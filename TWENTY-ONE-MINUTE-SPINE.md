# THE 21-MINUTE CUT — EIGHT SCENES TO ADD, AND WHERE

Option A confirmed: twelve clips, one per scene, 2,340. This adds eight more to reach 21
minutes — **1,560** — for a total of **3,900**.

Nothing here is padding. Each one closes a hole, sets up a payoff, or makes a death land.

---

## THE EIGHT

### 1 · THE VALUATION — 30s — *before the decision*
Ryan takes Armando's rough uncut diamond to a dealer's back room and watches the man's
face change. He is told a number. He does not react.

**Why:** the film never says what any of this is worth. Six people die for a sum the
audience never hears. This is the engine of the whole story and it is currently missing.
It also plants the buyer, so his return at the end is a payoff instead of a stranger.

### 2 · MATEO SAYS GOODBYE — 30s — *before the boatyard*
A doorway in the afternoon. Mateo, kit bag over his shoulder, crouches to his daughter's
height. Elena in the doorway behind, arms folded, not saying what she is thinking.

**Why:** Mateo is taken first and the audience has known him ninety seconds. This is the
single highest-value addition in the list — it makes the first death land, and it turns
the final doorway scene from a sad epilogue into a payoff the audience has been dreading
for eighteen minutes. **Same location, same actors, bookending the film.**

### 3 · UPRIVER — 30s — *after the dock, before the river mouth*
The boat running hard upriver at dawn. Seven people, gear lashed down, nobody talking.
Wide, then the island coming up out of the haze ahead.

**Why:** the film currently teleports from a dock to a jungle creek. This gives distance,
time, and one clean beautiful shot before it all goes wrong.

### 4 · NIGHT ONE — 30s — *before the settlement*
The team around a fire on the boat deck. Liam filming. Daniel counting out loud. Marcus
not joining in. Easy, ordinary, professional.

**Why:** six of these seven die. Right now they are strangers in olive shirts. Thirty
seconds of them being *fine* is what makes the rest cost something.

### 5–6 · THE 1974 CAMP — 60s (2 clips) — *after the radio*
A clearing that was a camp. Tent frames standing, canvas long gone. Wooden crates
stencilled with a company name and **1974**. And bones — human, not buried, just where
they fell, gone to moss. Maya finds the expedition log book. **Every page after the first
few has been torn out.**

**Why:** this was in the 42-minute script and cutting it is why the island has no history.
It gives the radio voice a source, it explains that everyone before them also went in, and
the torn pages say somebody in 1974 knew and refused to write it down. It is the best
worldbuilding in the script.

### 7 · THE ARGUMENT — 30s — *after Mateo dies*
Maya says they are turning back. Ryan says they are not. Marcus does not take a side, which
is its own answer.

**Why:** **this is the largest hole in the film.** Six professionals lose a man to something
invisible and simply keep walking. No audience believes that. Thirty seconds of somebody
saying the obvious thing out loud — and being overruled — makes Ryan culpable, which is what
the whole ending is built on.

### 8 · THE DRONE SEES HER — 30s — *before the flooded crossing*
Liam flies the drone. The team crowd round the screen. On it, in degraded low-resolution
grey, something very long moves under the water and out of frame.

**Why:** it is the only time the team knows what they are dealing with, and it hands the
audience the crossing sequence in advance — so when they wade in, we are the only ones who
understand what is under them. **It also uses the drone-shot still as the screen image.**
