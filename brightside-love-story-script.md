# Bright Side–Style Video Script #3 (Animated Love Story Genre)

**Working title:** I Fell in Love Through Notes in a Library Book — and He Was My Noisy Neighbor
**Alt titles (A/B thumbnails):**
- "I Wrote to a Stranger in a Library Book for Months. Then I Found Out Who He Was"
- "The Neighbor I Hated Was the Person I Was Falling For"

**Target length:** ~8.5 minutes
**Format:** First-person voiceover + 2D character animation, chaptered story (not a listicle)
**Tone:** Warm, funny, a little bittersweet — cozy rom-com energy, family-friendly

---

## Why this genre

Bright Side's animated first-person stories ("my story" format) live on
**relatability + a reveal**. Retention comes from a strong hook that teases the
ending, short chapters that each end on a mini-cliffhanger, a mid-video reveal the
*viewer* catches a beat before the narrator does, and a payoff that recontextualizes
the opening. Enemies-to-lovers with a hidden-identity twist is a proven engine.

**Characters (original):**
- **Maya (narrator, 27):** Night-shift nurse. Sleeps days. Dry sense of humor.
- **Theo (28):** Lives in the next apartment. Works days at a bike repair shop,
  plays piano badly at night. Writes in pencil, tiny handwriting, draws doodles.
- **Mrs. Okafor (70s):** Library volunteer who notices everything. Comic relief +
  quiet matchmaker.

## SCRIPT

### COLD OPEN — HOOK (0:00–0:18)
> **[VISUAL:** Maya standing in an apartment hallway, holding a worn paperback,
> fist raised to knock on a door. Freeze-frame.**]**

**MAYA (V.O.):**
For three months, I was falling for someone I'd never met — through pencil notes
in the margins of a library book. For those same three months, I was in a war with
the guy next door, who played the same terrible piano song every night while I was
trying to sleep. I thought those were two completely different stories.
I was very, very wrong.

---

### TITLE CARD (0:18–0:28)
> **[VISUAL:** Bright Side logo sting. Title card: "THE MARGIN" with a pencil
> scribbling a small heart.**]**

**MAYA (V.O.):**
Let me start at the beginning — the night I wrote my first angry note. And no,
it wasn't in the book.

---

### CHAPTER 1 — THE WALL (0:28–1:35)
> **[VISUAL:** Maya in scrubs collapsing into bed at 9 a.m. Blackout curtains.
> Cut to night: through the wall — "plink… plonk… PLONK."**]**

**MAYA (V.O.):**
I'm a night-shift nurse. That means I sleep while the rest of the world is awake,
and my days off are a mess of trying to be a normal human. So when someone moved
into the apartment next to mine and started playing piano — at 11 p.m., every
night, on my nights off — I was not thrilled.

And it wasn't even good piano. It was the same song, over and over, with the same
wrong note in the exact same spot. Every. Single. Time. I started waiting for the
mistake. I could *feel* it coming.

> **[VISUAL:** Sticky note on a door: "Some of us sleep. — 4B"**]**

So I did what any mature adult does. I left a sticky note on his door.
The next morning, there was one on mine: "Some of us are learning. Sorry. — 4C."
With a tiny doodle of a sad piano.

I did not find it cute. I want that on the record.

---

### CHAPTER 2 — THE BOOK (1:35–2:50)
> **[VISUAL:** Cozy public library. Maya pulls an old paperback off a shelf.
> Inside: faint pencil writing in the margins.**]**

**MAYA (V.O.):**
On my days off, I hid at the library — it was quiet, which my apartment was not.
One afternoon I borrowed an old novel, the kind with a cracked spine and a coffee
ring on page 40. And in the margins, someone had written notes. In pencil.
Tiny handwriting.

Next to a sad line about loneliness, they'd written: *"Too real. Who hurt this
author?"* And next to a description of the ocean: *"I've never seen the sea.
Someday."*

> **[VISUAL:** Maya hesitates, then pulls a pencil from her bag.**]**

I know. You're not supposed to write in library books. But I wrote back anyway,
right under theirs: *"The sea is louder than you think. Worth it, though."*

I returned the book and figured that was the end of it.

Quick question before we go on — have you ever found a note from a stranger?
In a book, on a bus, anywhere? Tell me in the comments. I need to know I'm not
the only one this happened to.

---

### CHAPTER 3 — THE CONVERSATION (2:50–4:05)
> **[VISUAL:** Montage — the book changing hands on the return cart; new pencil
> lines appearing; seasons shifting outside the library window.**]**

**MAYA (V.O.):**
Two weeks later, I checked the book out again. Just to see. And there it was —
a reply. *"Louder than what? Tell me everything."*

That's how it started. We used the book like a very slow group chat. I'd write;
I'd return it; a week or so later, there'd be an answer. We never wrote names.
They called me "Sea Person." I called them "Pencil."

> **[VISUAL:** Margin notes stacking up — doodles of a bicycle, a cat, a tiny piano.**]**

Pencil fixed things for a living — "mostly bicycles, occasionally my life." Pencil
was learning an instrument as an adult and was, quote, "embarrassingly bad at it."
Pencil wrote that the hardest part wasn't the practice — it was trying anyway when
you know people can hear you fail.

I read that one on a twelve-hour shift break, in a hospital stairwell, and I had to
put the book down for a second.

> **[VISUAL:** Mrs. Okafor at the desk, peering over her glasses as Maya returns
> the book for the fifth time.**]**

Mrs. Okafor at the front desk noticed. "That's a lot of times for one book, dear."
I said I was a slow reader. She said, "Mm-hm." With a *very* specific face.

---

### CHAPTER 4 — THE WRONG NOTE (4:05–5:20)
> **[VISUAL:** Night. Maya on the couch with the book. Through the wall:
> the same piano song.**]**

**MAYA (V.O.):**
One night, Pencil wrote something new. *"I'm learning a song for my grandma.
She used to hum it while she cooked. There's one note I keep missing — the high
one, right before the end. I've decided it's my nemesis."*

And Pencil had drawn it. A little musical staff. Five notes. One circled, with an
angry face next to it.

> **[VISUAL:** Maya slowly lowers the book. Through the wall — "plink… plonk…"
> She looks at the drawing. Looks at the wall. Looks back at the drawing.**]**

And right then — through the wall — I heard it. The song. The same song I'd been
listening to every night for two months. Getting closer to the end… closer…

And he missed the high note.

> **[VISUAL:** Comedic zoom on Maya's face. Record-scratch sound.**]**

Okay. You've probably figured it out already. I — a medical professional, trained to
notice symptoms — had not. Not until that exact second.

But stick around — because knowing who he was turned out to be the *easy* part.

---

### MID-VIDEO ENGAGEMENT (5:20–5:35)
> **[VISUAL:** Split screen — Maya on one side of the wall, Theo on the other,
> both unaware.**]**

**MAYA (V.O.):**
So what would you have done? Knock on his door right away? Or keep writing and
pretend you didn't know? Comment "KNOCK" or "WRITE." I'll tell you what I did —
and it was not the smart option.

---

### CHAPTER 5 — THE PANIC (5:35–6:40)
> **[VISUAL:** Maya pacing. A thought-bubble replay of every sticky note she
> left on his door — they get progressively grumpier.**]**

**MAYA (V.O.):**
I panicked. Because Pencil was kind and funny and brave about failing. And 4C was
the guy I'd left *six* passive-aggressive sticky notes for. One of them said,
"Maybe try a kazoo. Quieter."

He'd been learning a song for his grandmother. And I'd suggested a kazoo.

> **[VISUAL:** Maya writing in the book, erasing, writing again.**]**

So I chose the cowardly option. I kept writing. But I started to feel like a liar,
because I knew and he didn't. Then one week, Pencil wrote: *"I think I'd like to
meet you. Saturday, 2 p.m., library café. I'll bring the book. No pressure — if you
don't come, I'll understand, and I'll keep writing anyway."*

Saturday. That gave me four days to figure out how to explain that the person he
wanted to meet was also the kazoo lady.

---

### CHAPTER 6 — THE NIGHT BEFORE (6:40–7:30)
> **[VISUAL:** Friday night. Maya standing in her hallway — the cold-open shot,
> now in context. Fist raised. Through the door: the piano.**]**

**MAYA (V.O.):**
Which brings us back to where we started. Friday night, standing outside 4C, holding
the book, about to knock. And I couldn't. My hand just hung there.

And then, through the door, he started the song again. Getting closer to the end…
closer… and I heard myself say — out loud, through the door —

"It's an F-sharp. You keep playing an F."

> **[VISUAL:** Silence. The piano stops. Footsteps. The door opens. Theo —
> messy hair, grease on his hands from the bike shop, holding a pencil.**]**

He looked at me. He looked at the book in my hand. He looked at me again.

And he said, "…Sea Person?"

And I said, "Also 4B."

And he said, "The kazoo lady."

> **[VISUAL:** Long beat. Then both burst out laughing.**]**

---

### CHAPTER 7 — THE SEA (7:30–8:10)
> **[VISUAL:** Time-skip montage — the two of them at the library café on Saturday
> anyway; Maya sitting next to Theo at the piano, pointing at the keys; Mrs. Okafor
> giving a thumbs-up from the reference desk.**]**

**MAYA (V.O.):**
We still met on Saturday — because he said a promise is a promise. Turns out
Mrs. Okafor had figured it out weeks before we did; she'd seen us both check out the
same book, from the same address. She never said a word. She just started setting it
aside on the cart for us. Like a very patient little mail service.

> **[VISUAL:** A beach at sunset. Theo standing at the edge of the water, shoes
> in hand. Maya watching him.**]**

That summer, I took him to see the sea for the first time. He stood there for a long
time and then said, "You were right. It's louder than you think."

And yes — he finally learned the song. F-sharp and everything. He played it for his
grandma on her birthday. I was there. She hummed along.

---

### OUTRO + CTA (8:10–8:35)
> **[VISUAL:** Close-up of the old library book, now on their shared shelf.
> Final margin note in two different handwritings. Subscribe + bell animation.**]**

**MAYA (V.O.):**
We bought the book from the library, by the way. Mrs. Okafor insisted. On the last
page, there's one more note. His handwriting says: *"Same time next chapter?"*
Mine says: *"Every night. Even the wrong notes."*

So — be nice to your neighbors. You never know who's on the other side of the wall.

If this story made you smile, hit like, subscribe, and ring the bell for more
stories like this. And tell me in the comments: what's the most unexpected way
you've ever met someone?

## Production Notes
- **Chapter bumpers:** Replace countdown numbers with pencil-sketch chapter cards
  ("CHAPTER 3 — THE CONVERSATION") that "write themselves" on a page — same retention
  role as number bumpers.
- **The reveal:** Plant the piano clues early (same song, same wrong note, "learning
  an instrument," tiny-piano doodle on the sticky note) so attentive viewers solve it
  in Ch. 3 — letting them feel smart is the hook into Ch. 4. Keep Maya's realization
  as a comedic beat, not a shock.
- **Callbacks:** The cold-open freeze-frame returns in Ch. 6; "louder than you think"
  returns at the beach; the wrong note becomes the confession line. Every setup pays off.
- **Recurring audio motif:** Compose a short, simple original melody. Play it with the
  wrong note through Ch. 1–6, correctly for the first time in Ch. 7. This is the
  emotional payoff — mix it clearly.
- **Visual language:** Margin notes in two distinct handwriting styles (Maya: neat
  cursive; Theo: tiny print + doodles) so viewers can follow the "conversation."
- **Tone guardrail:** Wholesome and advertiser-safe. Conflict is comedic (sticky
  notes), never mean-spirited; the romance stays sweet, no explicit content.
- **Thumbnail:** Split image — Maya glaring at a wall on one side, Theo at a piano on
  the other, a library book bridging them; text: "IT WAS HIM?!"
- **Music:** Light, plucky strings for the feud; soft piano for the letters; full,
  warm swell at the beach scene.
- **Originality:** All characters, plot and the melody are original — no references to
  existing books, songs or films by name.
