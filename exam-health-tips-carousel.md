# Exam Health Tips — Instagram Carousel (8 slides)

Health-education carousel for Nnamdi Azikiwe University Medical Center, Awka.

## Design system

All eight slides share one visual system, anchored to Slide 1:

- **Format:** 1:1 square, 2048 × 2048 px
- **Background:** warm cream ivory, with a pale sage-green quarter-circle in the top-right corner
- **Badge:** dark forest-green pill, top-left, lightbulb icon + `EXAM HEALTH TIPS`
- **Headline:** large bold condensed sans-serif, dark forest green, left column
- **Accent:** short mustard-yellow bar under the headline
- **Body:** charcoal-grey regular sans-serif
- **Footer:** dark green circle + white chevron, `Swipe to see more tips` (slides 1–7)
- **Illustration:** flat 2D vector, clean dark outlines, flat shading, no gradients, right half
- **Recurring character:** the same young Black African male student throughout — warm brown skin, short curly black hair, sage-green t-shirt
- **Palette:** cream, forest green, sage green, mustard yellow, navy, warm brown

## Slide copy

### 1. Set realistic study targets.
Work for one academic-session cannot be forced into your brain in one night.

*Illustration: student at his desk writing in a notebook; books, mug, flip-chart reading "PLAN FOCUS ACHIEVE", wall clock, sticky note "Small goals Big results".*

### 2. Take study breaks.
Do not sit in one place for too long. Stretch your legs, rest your eyes and refresh your mind.

*Illustration: student stepping away from his desk, looking out of a bright window at a tree and sky.*

### 3. Get adequate sleep.
Avoid regular all-night studying. Sleep is important for memory consolidation and concentration.

*Illustration: student resting in bed under a forest-green blanket; nightstand lamp, alarm clock, crescent moon and stars through the window.*

### 4. Eat well and stay hydrated.
Do not rely on junk food. Your brain and body need balanced meals. Water reduces headaches and poor focus. Never skip breakfast on exam day.

*Illustration: student enjoying a balanced meal; vegetables, carrots, rice and fish, a fruit bowl, a glass of water and a jug.*

### 5. Avoid quick fixes and energy boosters.
No energy drinks, alcohol or drugs to manage stress. Limit caffeine. They cause anxiety, insomnia and sudden crashes in alertness.

*Illustration: student declining an energy drink and coffee cup marked with prohibition symbols, choosing a glass of water instead.*

### 6. Seek medical attention early.
Visit the Medical Center as soon as you notice a decline in your health. Do not wait until it worsens.

*Illustration: student in a friendly consultation with a doctor in a white coat with a stethoscope and clipboard; green medical cross on the wall.*

### 7. Manage existing conditions.
Keep your medications fully stocked and follow all medical advice. Tell the examiner early if the hall seating may aggravate your condition.

*Illustration: student packing a labelled medication box and inhaler into a clear pouch with his pen and ID card; weekly pill organiser, wall calendar with a circled date.*

### 8. We wish you all the best in your exams.
Stay healthy. Stay focused.

The Medical Team,
Nnamdi Azikiwe University Medical Center, Awka.

*Illustration: student walking confidently into the examination hall on exam day with a satchel and a thumbs up; morning sunlight and celebratory sparkles. Footer swaps the swipe prompt for a health icon and the Medical Center name.*
