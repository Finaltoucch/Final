# DIAMOND ANACONDA — Story Structure Rebuild

**Logline:** Seven retired operators go to a forbidden island to steal a fortune in
diamonds, and discover too late that the diamonds are eggs — and that the thing
guarding them is not hunting the men, but the treasure in their packs.

**Format:** Narrated / animated creature-feature short film
**Target runtime:** 42 minutes (variants for 20 and 70 at the end of this doc)
**Structure:** 7 movements — mystery → character → discovery → escalating horror →
sacrifice → final confrontation → consequence
**Tone:** Grounded survival horror. A real animal, not a monster. No jump-scare
stingers — dread built from silence, water, and the sound of something heavy moving.
**Theme:** *Everything valuable belongs to something.*

---

## 1. What changed, and why

The original treatment is a chronology: the team arrives, snakes attack, people die,
Ryan escapes. Each beat is good in isolation, but nothing a character learns in
scene 5 changes what they can do in scene 9. That's what makes it read as a
sequence of attacks rather than a film. Five structural fixes:

**Fix 1 — The diamonds are eggs.**
The single change that turns the creature into a character. Currently she is
territorial scenery. As a mother defending a clutch, she has a want, a reason to
appear when she appears, and a reason to stop. It also converts the treasure from
a goal into a *liability* — which is the engine of the entire third act.

The regurgitation legend survives intact, as the story the old man tells. It's what
the islanders believe. It's wrong. The audience learns the truth twenty minutes
after they've accepted the myth, which is the best possible time to take it away.

**Fix 2 — A rule the audience can play along with.**
She doesn't track heat or sound. She tracks her eggs. Establish it by accident
(Deke's body), confirm it under pressure (Maya), weaponize it in the finale (Ryan).
Once the audience knows the rule, every shot of a man with a full pack is a
countdown, and horror comes from *anticipation* instead of surprise.

**Fix 3 — Every death hands the survivors something.**
Six deaths, six inheritances: charges, footage, time, the clue, the key, the trigger.
This is the difference between attrition and escalation. Nobody dies just to lower
the headcount.

**Fix 4 — A real sacrifice, with information attached.**
Your outline has no sacrifice beat — people are taken, they don't choose. Maya
chooses, and her last transmission is the rule Ryan needs. Sacrifice that also
delivers plot is the beat audiences remember.

**Fix 5 — The opening asks a question instead of showing a threat.**
Details in §3. Your cold open ends with a lunge; the audience learns "there's a
big snake," which the title already told them. Rebuilt, it ends with a man doing
something inexplicable, and the audience carries that question for forty minutes.

## 2. The engine

**The mystery (planted 0:00, answered 0:29):** What are the diamonds actually *for*?

**The rules — established, confirmed, exploited:**
1. She follows the eggs, not the men. Drop the stone, drop the hunt.
2. The eggs are cold. Human hands warm them. A warmed egg is a beacon.
3. She will not damage a chamber holding a clutch. The deepest chamber is the one
   place she cannot strike freely — and the one place Ryan can bring the roof down.

**The moral machine:** Every man who keeps a diamond dies. Every man who drops one
lives a little longer. Ryan survives because he finally puts them down — and the
last scene exists because he went back for three.

## 6. Scene-by-scene

### I — MYSTERY

**1. The legend (0:02–0:05)**
Coastal settlement, dusk. The OLD MAN, a fire, an ancient photograph of a
jungle-covered island. He tells it as gospel: for centuries, an enormous serpent
has lived beneath that island. She goes down into the dark, and she comes back up
and *brings up diamonds from her belly.* They call her the mother serpent.

Ryan doesn't believe a word of it. The old man puts a small glittering stone on the
table. Ryan turns it in the firelight. Real.

> OLD MAN: "That is the smallest one."

*Play the scene so the audience believes the myth completely. It is the version of
the truth that lets them be ambushed later. Note for the rewatch: the old man says
"she brings them up" — not "she makes them." He's not lying. He's just a man
repeating what his grandfather told him.*

### II — CHARACTER

**2. The seven (0:05–0:09)**
A rented boathouse. Gear on the floor. Introduce each with one action, no résumés:
Boyd cleaning a rifle he's owned for thirty years · Sal taping charges and telling
someone about his daughter's braces · Wren under a loupe with the old man's stone,
already unhappy with what she's seeing · Deke counting the split out loud and
getting it wrong on purpose to needle Ryan · Eli filming everyone and being told
to stop · Maya packing four times the blood-clotting agent the job should need.

Wren, to nobody: *"Diamonds don't form like this. This didn't come out of the
ground."* Nobody listens. **Planted at 0:07, detonated at 0:29.**

Maya and Ryan alone on the dock. She tells him this is her last one and makes him
say it back. He says it. He means it when he says it.

> RYAN: "We go in, we find the diamonds, we get out."
> DEKE: "What could possibly go wrong?"

### III — DISCOVERY

**3. The island (0:09–0:12)**
Aerial: enormous, green, black water, mangrove channels, no structures, no smoke.
The boat enters the river. Light goes flat. The engine dies on its own.

Then the silence lands — no birds, no insects, nothing. And in the mangroves above
them: shed skins. Not one. Dozens, hanging like laundry, some of them thirty feet
long. Nobody speaks for a long beat.

> RYAN: "We're being watched."

**4. First blood (0:12–0:15)**
Camp at an abandoned settlement — rotted crates, a collapsed dock, a village that
was left in a hurry decades ago. Maya finds a military field radio with charge still
in it, which is impossible. She switches it on. Static. Then a voice, faint,
close-mic'd, exhausted:

> *"—don't go underground—"*

The transmission cuts. Nobody can raise it again. (*Pay this at 0:33: the voice is
a woman's, and the last thing Maya does is key a mic in the same dead channel.*)

That night Sal is taken. His weapon is lying at the waterline, safety still on.
No blood, no drag marks on the bank — just a wide, smooth trough in the mud going
into black water. Ryan sees a shape pass under the surface, longer than the boat,
and it's gone. **Sal's charge bag stays in the camp inventory.**

**5. The island is alive (0:15–0:18)**
Deeper. Tracks in the mud that are not tracks — they're impressions, four feet
across, with no start and no end.

The 1974 camp: rotted tents, expedition crates, photographs of eleven men, human
bones arranged in no particular way. And a notebook.

> *"The diamonds are real."*
> *"But the diamonds aren't what killed us."*

Ryan turns the page. The rest has been torn out.

Then the canopy comes alive — a snake drops, then three more. Gunfire, chaos, the
team survives it and understands something worse: there isn't one snake. There are
hundreds. Eli's drone, up and panicking, catches four seconds of something moving
under the canopy from one side of the frame to the other and never showing its end.
He's taken in the retreat. **The drone comes down in shallow water, still recording.**

### IV — ESCALATION

**6. The descent (0:21–0:26)**
The sinkhole. The cave from the legend. Their lights find the walls and the walls
find them back — ten thousand points of light, diamonds seamed through the rock in
clusters. For thirty seconds these are the richest people alive and the film lets
them enjoy it.

Deeper: a wall of carvings. Thousands of serpent marks made by human hands over
centuries — offerings, tallies, warnings. At the center, one enormous carved coil.

> RYAN: "This isn't where it lives."
> WREN: "Then what is it?"
> RYAN: "It's where it *comes back to.*"

**7. The clue (0:26–0:29)**
Deke is found in a side passage. Alone. Untouched — no wound, no constriction
bruising, he simply ran until his heart stopped. In his jacket, wrapped in a sock:
a diamond the size of an orange, taken from the wall and hidden from the others.

Boyd, flatly: *"He was the only one carrying one loose."*
Nobody wants to follow the thought.

**8. Wren cracks it (0:29–0:31)**
Wren has the stone under a light. It's not a crystal lattice. It's *layered* —
concentric, like nacre. There's a soft mass at the center. It's slightly warm on
the side that was against Deke's ribs.

> WREN: "It's not a mineral. It's a shell."
> MAYA: "A shell around what?"

Beat.

> WREN: "She doesn't bring them up. She *lays* them."

The team is standing in a nest, in the dark, with sixty pounds of her young
distributed across their packs. **Every question from the cold open now has a
shape, and the audience is ahead of the characters for the first time.**

**9. Trapped (0:31–0:33)**
The hatchlings come — small, fast, in numbers, and *coordinated*, driving the team
along passages rather than swarming them. Boyd puts himself in a chokepoint with
the rifle and the last two magazines and tells Ryan to go, without drama, because
he's been waiting thirty years for a reason to. Eleven minutes of shooting, then
silence.

They run for the river. The river is gone — flooded to the roof.

> RYAN: "We're not hunting the snake anymore."
> *(pause)*
> "It's hunting us."

### V — SACRIFICE

**10. The flood gate (0:33–0:36)**
One way out: a sluice passage under the old settlement, holding back the flood on a
century-old timber gate. Someone has to stay on the wrong side and hold the winch
while the others cross.

Maya takes it before Ryan can. Not a speech — she just puts her pack down, which
is when Ryan understands, because *she's dropping the diamonds.*

> MAYA: "You said it back to me. On the dock."
> RYAN: "Maya—"
> MAYA: "So go home and be someone who says things and means them."

She holds the gate. On the radio — the same dead channel as the voice from
night one — she narrates what she's watching come down the passage toward her,
calm, clinical, right to the end:

> MAYA: "Ryan. She went past me. She didn't even slow down."
> *(static)*
> "She's not following us. She's following what we took. **Drop them.**"

The gate goes. **The rule is now in Ryan's hands. He does not drop his pack.**

### VI — CONFRONTATION

**11. The deep chamber (0:36–0:38)**
Ryan and Wren reach the bottom. The chamber is vast and the walls are solid light —
a sky of diamonds. And in a hollow at the center, arranged, deliberate: the clutch.
Hundreds.

Wren is looking at the answer to her entire career, and she starts laughing, and
then she can't stop, and then the far wall *moves* — two thousand years of animal
uncoiling, so slowly that it reads as geology before it reads as alive.

Wren fires. Every round, into an animal the size of a train, in a stone room.
The chamber answers. Wren is gone.

**12. Ryan stops running (0:38–0:41)**
He can't outrun her and now he knows he doesn't have to. He has three things:
Sal's charges, the rule, and the one place she will not strike — over her own clutch.

He rigs the roof. He uses the water, the narrow passages, her own length against
her — she is enormous, and enormous things cannot turn in a corridor. Cat and mouse,
not a shooting gallery.

She corners him anyway. He falls. Her jaws open —

**And we are in the cold open.** Same tunnel. Same handprints. Ryan takes the
fist-sized egg out of his pack, sets it down on the wet stone, and backs away —
and her head follows *the stone,* exactly as it did forty minutes ago, and now the
audience knows why. The daylight behind him is the wrong way, because the charges
are the other way.

> RYAN (whisper): "We were never supposed to find them."

He runs deeper. The charges go. The chamber comes down on two thousand years of her.
Ryan makes the surface with the roof coming down behind him.

### VII — CONSEQUENCE

**13. The return (0:41)**
Morning. Ryan comes out of the treeline caked in mud and blood and sits down in the
boat. No music swell. No relief. He opens his pack.

Three enormous diamonds. Sunlight hits them.

He looks at them for a long time — long enough for the audience to count six people
against three stones. His hands start to shake. He closes the bag.

**14. Final scene (0:42)**
A dealer's back office. One stone on black velvet. The BUYER's face changes.

> BUYER: "Where did you get this?"

Ryan says nothing.

> BUYER: "There's no mine on earth that produces something like this."

Ryan takes the money and walks.

That night, he wakes. A sound in the dark room. A hiss — small, thin, close.

He looks at the table. The diamond is still there. But inside it now there's a thin
dark line that wasn't there before.

He gets closer.

**The line moves.**

He steps back. Somewhere out past the window, very far away, something enormous
answers.

CUT TO BLACK. **END.**

## 8. Runtime variants

**20-minute cut:** Drop to five operators (fold Sal's charges into Boyd, cut Eli and
the drone). Lose the 1974 camp; move the notebook into the first cave. Keep every
beat of movements I, V, VI, VII intact — the sacrifice and the cold-open resolution
are the film.

**42-minute cut:** As written.

**70-minute cut:** Expand three places, and only these three. (a) The boathouse —
give Deke and Wren an argument about what the stone is worth that establishes both
characters and the theme. (b) Night two, before Deke's body — a long, quiet
sequence of the team realizing their map is wrong, no creature on screen at all.
(c) The 1974 camp — find one survivor's remains sealed inside a chamber *from the
inside,* with a diamond he refused to give up. Do not expand the attacks; adding
creature screen time is exactly what makes this genre sag.

**Optional post-credit (recommend against, but it's yours):** Eli's drone, half-buried
in silt, battery on 1%, still recording. Something moves past the lens. Cut. It's a
good shot, but the final diamond is the better last image — a second ending divides
the one you've got.

## 9. Production notes

**The creature.** Never show her whole body before 0:18, and when you do, do it via
Eli's drone footage — degraded, wide, four seconds. Full clean reveal is reserved
for 0:37 in the deep chamber. Real-animal reference throughout: green anaconda
musculature and movement, scaled up, with the slowness that real mass implies.
Nothing should move faster than physics allows. She is never malicious. She is a
mother, and that is worse.

**Sound.** The film lives on three sounds: dripping water, a heavy body displacing
water, and silence where insects should be. Establish "no insects" at 0:10 loudly
enough that the audience misses it later without being told.

**Light.** Above ground: flat, overcast, colorless. Below ground: only what the team
is carrying — and diamond-scatter, which should be genuinely beautiful. The most
gorgeous images in the film should be in the most dangerous room. That contrast is
the whole thesis.

**Palette discipline.** Green world / black water / white light / one recurring hot
colour reserved exclusively for the diamonds. When the last shot needs the dark line
to read instantly, the audience will already be trained on that colour.

**Deaths.** Only Wren's death happens fully on camera. Everything else is: the
moment before, then absence. Cheaper to produce, and considerably more frightening.
