# The Framework — Taken From Channels That Work

Not invented. This is the documented structure of two real channels, combined
into a template for ours.

**Source A — Company Man** · 1.79M subs · business explainers · the *structure*
**Source B — Improvement Pill** · 3.77M subs · stick figure animation · the *teaching method*

**Date:** 2026-08-02

---

## Source A — Company Man's Structure

Documented format:

1. **One subject per video.** One company, one narrative arc. **No anthologies,
   no listicles.** Every video is a single story.
2. **The hook sets up the FALL before describing the RISE — inside the first 15
   seconds.**
3. **Documentary three-act arc:** problem → conflict → resolution.
4. **No custom animation, no motion graphics.** Licensed music and well-edited
   stock footage. That's the entire visual stack.
5. **Research-heavy scripts, voiceover only, no on-camera host.**

Reported result: this documentary structure **holds viewers past the midpoint
better than most YouTube content categories.** Midpoint retention is the metric
that decides whether a video gets pushed, so this is the load-bearing finding.

### The one device worth stealing outright

**Fall before rise, in the first 15 seconds.**

Not "here's a company that got big." It's *"this company was worth forty-seven
billion dollars. Eighteen months later it was worth nothing. Here's how that
happened."*

The ending is given away immediately, and that's exactly why people stay — the
question stops being *what happens* and becomes *how*. "How" is a much stronger
hook than "what," because it can't be satisfied by a thumbnail.

### And the finding that should shape everything

**No custom animation. 1.79 million subscribers.**

Company Man wins on topic selection and narrative structure while running the
cheapest visual stack on the platform. Our stick figures will already be more
distinctive than the market leader's visuals. **The visuals are not where this is
won.** Do not let animation eat the time that belongs to research and writing.

## Source B — Improvement Pill's Method

3.77M subscribers on stick figure animation delivering serious instructional
content.

The documented core: **stories are the teaching vehicle.** Not illustrated
explanation — narrative. The creator teaches storytelling professionally
(Skillshare class, and a paid formula sold to other creators), and credits story
structure for the channel's growth.

Applied to our niche: don't explain that printer ink is overpriced. **Follow
someone buying a printer**, and let the economics reveal themselves as it happens
to them.

That's what makes stick figures work in a serious niche. A figure isn't
decoration on top of an explanation — the figure is the *protagonist*, and the
viewer follows a character rather than absorbing a lecture.

> **Note on a discrepancy:** the Skillshare bio cites 2.5M subscribers, current
> analytics show 3.77M. The bio is simply older.

## The Combined Template

| Section | Time | What happens | Visual |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The fall** | 0:00–0:15 | Give away the ending as a number. Company Man's device. No greeting, no channel name. | One figure, one shocking number, hard cut |
| **2. The character** | 0:15–1:00 | Introduce the stick figure this happens *to*. Improvement Pill's device. Make it a person, not a topic. | Figure enters, ordinary situation |
| **3. The setup** | 1:00–2:30 | Normal world. What the character believes is true. Plant the assumption you'll break. | Simple scene, calm, no red accent yet |
| **4. The turn** | 2:30–4:00 | The first number that doesn't add up. **First use of oxblood.** | Red enters the frame for the first time |
| **5. The mechanism** | 4:00–7:30 | How the system actually works. Three beats, each: claim → sourced number → consequence for the character. | Scale contrast shots, diagrams |
| **6. The scale** | 7:30–9:00 | Zoom out from one person to the whole market. The number that makes it matter. | Many figures, or one giant object |
| **7. The callback** | 9:00–10:00 | Return to the opening number. Now it means something different. One action. Earn the sub. | Return to the opening composition |

**Total ~10 minutes.** Visual changes every 3–5 seconds inside those sections.

### Why this ordering

- Section 1 is the only thing that gets someone to click and stay past 15 seconds
- Section 2 is what makes them care — an abstract topic can't be rooted for
- Section 4 lands the first "wait, what" at roughly the 25% mark, which is where
  most viewers quit
- Section 6 answers "why should I care" before the close, not after
- Section 7 gives the payoff the hook promised, which is what earns a subscribe

## Applied — Video 1

**Why Printer Ink Costs More Than Champagne**

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **The fall** | "A litre of printer ink costs more than a litre of Dom Pérignon. Not a bit more. Ten times more. And roughly half of it will never touch a page." |
| **The character** | A figure buys a printer. Seventy-nine dollars. Feels like a bargain. |
| **The setup** | It is a bargain. The manufacturer is losing money on it — and that's the plan. |
| **The turn** | The first cartridge. First oxblood in the frame. |
| **The mechanism** | ① Razor-and-blades: hardware sold at or below cost, consumables carry the margin. ② Ink runs $440–$3,200 per litre against champagne at ~$270. ③ Consumer Reports: **over half your ink can be spent on self-cleaning cycles** before printing anything. |
| **The scale** | Figure surrounded by every printer they'll own in a lifetime. |
| **The callback** | Back to the champagne bottle. Now with the total. |

**Every number above is sourced.** That's the product.
