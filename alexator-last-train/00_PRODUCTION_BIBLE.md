# LAST TRAIN — Production Bible

**Client:** ALEXATOR · **Track:** *All for You* (017) · **Delivered:** 15 September 2026
**Brief:** ALEXATOR — AI Short Video Creative Brief

## Concept

Past midnight, a young woman falls asleep on the last train home, still in her work clothes.
A conductor near the end of his own shift works down the aisle, reaches her, and reads the
destination on the ticket still loose in her hand.

Then he does the thing the film is about: **he does not wake her.** He straightens up, takes
the grab pole, and waits — giving her the last few minutes and carrying the responsibility
himself. When the train slows he leans down and wakes her at her stop. She gathers her bag
and goes. As the train pulls away he lifts one hand, barely, and is gone.

**The subject:** almost all kindness is invisible and unrepaid. He did more than the job asked,
for a stranger who can never return it, and neither of them made anything of it.

## Differentiation from the previous three films

| | RIDE HOME | FORGE | THE LONG WORK | LAST TRAIN |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Who | boy, ~10 | woman, early 30s | woman, late 50s | **two people** |
| When | warm afternoon | night | cold dawn | night |
| Where | suburban street | industrial interior | open hillside | **train interior, in motion** |
| Colour | golden pastel | orange on black | wet green/grey | **cold green-white / sodium orange** |
| Shape | travel | making an object | repetition → reveal | **one small decision** |

This is the first of the four with **two characters**. That was the largest unused axis: the
previous three are each one person alone, so the film is about what passes *between* people
rather than what one person does.

## Realism doctrine

A train, rain, a ticket, a wristwatch, two tired people. Nothing supernatural, nothing
impossible. Rain on glass, wet reflective surfaces and doubled reflections in dark windows are
among the strongest subjects for this generation of models.

**Deliberate rule: no signage, destination display, advertising or lettering in any frame.**
AI-generated text renders mangled and the brief bans on-screen text, so the conductor's moment
of decision is his face and a wristwatch, never an LED readout.

## Consistency anchors

Three reference sheets, each generated once and passed as `image_references` into the keyframes:

1. **Character — the young woman.** Early twenties, exhausted, low ponytail, plain navy work
   polo with no logo, oversized charcoal wool coat, olive canvas tote.
2. **Character — the conductor.** Mid sixties, grey moustache, plain navy uniform with **no
   badge, crest or insignia**, reading glasses on a cord, plain ticket machine on a strap.
3. **Location — the carriage.** Photographed from both ends: same moquette, seat layout, grab
   poles, ceiling light strip, window spacing and worn flooring, so eleven separate generations
   read as one continuous train.

The third sheet is the lesson from the FORGE spiral and THE LONG WORK terrain applied where the
risk actually sat on this film.

## Prohibited-content compliance

- Both characters entirely invented; no real-person reference of any kind used.
- No logos, brands, trademarks, operator markings or insignia anywhere.
- No signage, screens, displays, advertising or readable text in any frame.
- No stock footage or third-party visual material.
