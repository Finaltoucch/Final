# Story Script #3 — Loyalty / Karma Drama ("The Maid Who Stayed" genre)

**Working title:** Everyone Left the Dying Millionaire — Only His Housekeeper Stayed. Then the Will Was Read…
**Alt titles (A/B thumbnails):**
- "His Family Abandoned Him for His Money — The Housekeeper Didn't"
- "They Laughed at the Housekeeper… Until the Lawyer Said Her Name"
- "The Housekeeper Who Stayed When Everyone Left"

**Reference video:** [The Maid Who Stayed When Everyone Left!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ypf96Mka05Q)
**Setting:** Nashville, Tennessee (fully American cast, locations, and details)
**Target length:** ~14–16 minutes (~2,200 words of narration)
**Format:** Single narrator (warm, slightly dramatic, American accent) over cinematic
AI-generated stills / light motion, with short in-character dialogue lines
**Tone:** Emotional, slow-burn injustice → vindication. Viewer should feel angry at
the family by minute 3 and satisfied by the end.

---

## Style Breakdown (what this genre does, and how this script copies it)

| Element | How the genre does it | Where it shows up here |
|---|---|---|
| **Title/thumbnail** | Contrast: humble helper vs. rich family, and a promised payoff ("then…") | Titles above; thumbnail = housekeeper in uniform, crying family behind her, lawyer holding a will |
| **Cold-open hook** | Open on the *climax*, then rewind ("But to understand how we got here…") | 0:00 will-reading flash-forward |
| **Stakes stacking** | Every scene takes something from the hero *and* makes the villains nastier | Pay cut → fired → called a thief → locked out |
| **Villain clarity** | Villains say the quiet part out loud, with no ambiguity | Vanessa and Bradley's dialogue |
| **Mini-hooks** | A cliffhanger line every 60–90s so viewers don't click away | Marked **[RETENTION HOOK]** |
| **Mid-video engagement** | Narrator asks viewers to comment | 6:30 and the ending |
| **The twist** | The "helpless" rich man knew more than anyone thought | Thomas's recorder + secret lawyer visits |
| **Karma payoff** | Villains humiliated in public, hero rewarded, hero stays humble | Will reading + epilogue |
| **Moral + CTA** | One-sentence lesson, then like/subscribe | Closing |

**Editing notes (apply throughout):**
- A new image every 3–5 seconds. Slow push-ins (Ken Burns) on emotional lines.
- Tense moments: soft piano + low drone. Twist reveals: 0.5s silence, then a hit.
- Burn the key villain lines into on-screen captions (big white text, yellow keyword).
- Warm golden grade for Grace, cold blue/gray grade for the family.

## SCRIPT

### COLD OPEN — FLASH-FORWARD HOOK (0:00–0:35)
> **[VISUAL:** Wood-paneled law office. A family in designer clothes leaning forward,
> smiling. At the back, by the door, a woman in a faded blue cardigan clutches her purse.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Everyone in that room was waiting to hear how many millions they'd get. The wife was
already picking out a new house. The son had already promised his friends a boat.

And standing by the door, the one person nobody had bothered to offer a chair,
was Grace Miller, the housekeeper. She expected nothing. She wasn't even sure why
she'd been invited.

> **[VISUAL:** Lawyer adjusts glasses, unfolds a document. Close-up on the family's smiles.**]**

Then the lawyer cleared his throat and read the first name. It wasn't the wife's,
and it wasn't the son's.

> **[SFX:** Silence. Single piano note.**]**

To understand why that room went dead silent, we have to go back eleven months to the
night Thomas Caldwell collapsed on his kitchen floor.

> **[VISUAL:** Title card: *THE HOUSEKEEPER WHO STAYED*]**

---

### ACT 1 — THE PERFECT FAMILY (0:35–2:30)
> **[VISUAL:** Aerial of a Belle Meade mansion in Nashville. White columns, magnolia trees.
> Thomas, 64, silver hair, laughing at a barbecue.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Thomas Caldwell built Caldwell Freight from one used truck and a loan from his mother.
Forty years later, he had 300 trucks across six states and a house in Nashville
everyone slowed down to look at.

He also had a family who loved him, or at least who loved the life he paid for.

> **[VISUAL:** Vanessa, 41, blonde, sunglasses, champagne glass. Bradley, 34, on his phone.**]**

Vanessa was his second wife. She was twenty-three years younger than him and much more
interested in his credit cards than his conversation. Bradley, his son from his first
marriage, had "worked" at the company for ten years. Nobody could remember him doing
anything there.

> **[VISUAL:** Grace, 56, in the kitchen before sunrise, making biscuits. Warm light.**]**

And then there was Grace. She was a widow from a small town in Ohio who had cleaned that
house for nineteen years. She knew Thomas took his coffee black with one ice cube,
because he was always too impatient to let it cool. She knew he hummed Johnny Cash
when he was worried. She was the only person in that house who ever asked him how his
day went.

**THOMAS** *(smiling, taking the coffee)*:
"Grace, you're the only one around here who doesn't want anything from me."

**GRACE:**
"Oh, I want something, Mr. Caldwell. I want you to eat a vegetable once a week."

> **[VISUAL:** Thomas laughing. Freeze frame.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
It was the last time anyone heard him laugh for a very long time.

> **[RETENTION HOOK]**

---

### ACT 2 — THE COLLAPSE (2:30–4:15)
> **[VISUAL:** Night. Kitchen. Shattered coffee mug. Thomas on the tile floor.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
It was a Tuesday night in October. Grace had stayed late to finish the ironing. She
heard the mug break first, and then the thud.

Thomas had had a massive stroke. Grace called 911, kept him on his side, and held his
hand for eleven minutes until the paramedics arrived. She kept telling him:
*"Stay with me, Tom. You stay with me."*

> **[VISUAL:** Grace alone in a hospital hallway at 3 a.m. Vending-machine light.**]**

Vanessa was in Miami with friends. She texted back the next morning: *"Keep me posted."*
Bradley showed up two days later and spent most of the visit asking the doctor one
question:

**BRADLEY:**
"So… realistically… is he going to be able to make decisions again?"

> **[VISUAL:** Doctor's uneasy face. Caption: *"Is he going to be able to make decisions again?"*]**

**NARRATOR:**
Thomas survived, but the stroke took his speech and most of the movement on his right
side. The doctors said recovery was possible, but it would be slow and hard, and it
would take someone who wouldn't give up on him.

The family had already given up.

> **[RETENTION HOOK]**

---

### ACT 3 — EVERYONE LEAVES (4:15–7:00)
> **[VISUAL:** Thomas in a wheelchair being wheeled into a small back room, formerly storage.
> The master bedroom door closing behind Vanessa.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
When Thomas came home, Vanessa had him moved into the old storage room by the laundry.
She said the master bedroom "smelled like a hospital."

Within a month, she'd let the nurse go.

**VANESSA:**
"Two hundred dollars a day to sit with him? He doesn't even know what's going on."

> **[VISUAL:** Nurse leaving with a box. Then the gardener. Then the driver.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Then she let go of the gardener, then the driver, then the cook. One by one, the
people who'd worked in that house for years packed their things and left. Most of them
didn't argue. Tennessee is an at-will state, and everyone knew who was running things
now.

Finally Vanessa walked into the kitchen, where Grace was mashing sweet potatoes so
Thomas could swallow them.

**VANESSA:**
"We're cutting your hours. Actually, we're cutting your pay. Half. Take it or leave it."

**GRACE** *(quietly)*:
"Who's going to feed him? Who's going to do his exercises?"

**VANESSA** *(already walking away)*:
"Honestly, Grace? Not my problem."

> **[VISUAL:** Grace looking toward the back room. Close-up of her hands on the counter.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Grace could have walked out. She had a job offer from a family in Franklin paying twice
as much. Her own knees ached every night. Her daughter back in Ohio kept asking her to
come home.

She stayed anyway.

> **[VISUAL:** Montage, warm grade. Grace spoon-feeding Thomas. Grace moving his fingers
> one by one. Grace holding up flashcards: A… B… C…**]**

Every morning she did his physical therapy exercises, the ones she'd learned by
watching the nurse and then YouTube videos at night. Every afternoon she read him the
sports page and the Titans scores. And every night she sat by his bed and hummed Johnny
Cash until he fell asleep.

Nobody else in the house came into that room for weeks.

> **[ENGAGEMENT — on-screen text]**

**NARRATOR:**
Would you have stayed? Be honest, and tell us in the comments. Then keep watching,
because what Grace found in that room changed everything.

> **[RETENTION HOOK]**

---

### ACT 4 — THE ACCUSATION (7:00–9:15)
> **[VISUAL:** Night. Grace passing the study. Bradley and Vanessa whispering over papers.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
In February, Grace walked past the study late one night and heard her name.

**BRADLEY:**
"Once the power of attorney goes through, we sell the company. Buyer's already lined up."

**VANESSA:**
"And the old man goes to that place in Memphis. The cheap one."

**BRADLEY:**
"What about the housekeeper? She's always in there with him. She might tell someone."

**VANESSA** *(smiling)*:
"Then we make sure nobody believes her."

> **[SFX:** Low drone. Slow push-in on Vanessa's smile.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Three days later, Vanessa's diamond bracelet went missing.

> **[VISUAL:** Police cruiser in the driveway. Grace in the foyer, pale. Her purse turned
> upside down on the marble floor. The bracelet falls out.**]**

It turned up in Grace's purse, right where Vanessa said it would be.

**GRACE** *(shaking)*:
"I have never taken a single thing from this house. Not in nineteen years."

**VANESSA** *(to the officer)*:
"I don't want to press charges. I just want her *out*. Today."

> **[VISUAL:** Grace carrying one cardboard box down the driveway. Rain. She turns and
> looks up at the small back window.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Grace was escorted off the property in front of the neighbors. She never got to say
goodbye to Thomas.

But as she was walked past the back room, she saw his left hand, the one that still
worked, pressed flat against the window glass. And she could have sworn he was trying
to tell her something.

> **[RETENTION HOOK — hold on the hand for 2 seconds, then cut to black]**

---

### ACT 5 — WHAT THOMAS KNEW (9:15–11:30)
> **[VISUAL:** Grace's tiny rented apartment in East Nashville. A knock at the door.
> An older man in a gray suit holding a briefcase.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Two weeks later, there was a knock on Grace's door. The man standing there introduced
himself as Harold Jennings. He'd been Thomas Caldwell's attorney for thirty years.

**HAROLD:**
"Mrs. Miller, Tom asked me to find you. He's been trying to reach you for weeks."

**GRACE:**
"Mr. Caldwell? He can't even… he can't talk."

**HAROLD** *(sliding a small device across the table)*:
"He can now. A little. Thanks to you."

> **[VISUAL:** Close-up: a small digital voice recorder.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Here's what nobody in that house knew. All those months of flashcards and finger
exercises and humming had worked. Slowly, painfully, Thomas had gotten some of his
words back. He'd hidden it on purpose, because the moment he heard his wife and son
talking about "the cheap place in Memphis," he realized he couldn't trust anyone but
Grace.

Harold had been visiting him in secret while the family was out. Thomas had asked
him to leave a recorder in the room. His son, and the whole house, had assumed he
couldn't understand anything, so they'd said everything in front of him.

Harold pressed play.

> **[SFX:** Tinny recording. Vanessa's voice.**]**

**VANESSA (on recording):**
"…put the bracelet in her purse Thursday, call the cops Friday. She's gone by the
weekend…"

> **[VISUAL:** Grace's hand over her mouth. Tears.**]**

**HAROLD:**
"There's more. Tom updated his will in January. He wants you to be there when it's
read."

**GRACE:**
"When it's… is he—"

**HAROLD** *(gently)*:
"He's alive, Mrs. Miller. He's very much alive. But his family doesn't know that
the will has already changed. And he'd like it to stay that way until the right
moment."

> **[RETENTION HOOK]**

---

### ACT 6 — THE WILL READING (11:30–14:00)
> **[VISUAL:** Back to the law office from the cold open, same framing. Family seated,
> smiling. Grace by the door.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
And that brings us back to where we started.

Vanessa and Bradley had filed for power of attorney. They were so sure Thomas's mind
was gone that when Harold called a "meeting about the estate," they assumed he was
finally dying and came running. Vanessa wore black, just in case.

**HAROLD:**
"Before we begin, the first beneficiary: Mrs. Grace Miller."

> **[SFX:** Record scratch / hard silence.**]**

**VANESSA** *(laughing)*:
"The *maid*? The maid who stole from us?"

**HAROLD:**
"About that."

> **[VISUAL:** Harold presses play on a laptop. The recording echoes through the room.
> Bradley's face drains. Vanessa's smile freezes.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
The recording played through the whole room: the bracelet, the fake police report,
the buyer for the company, and the cheap place in Memphis.

Then the door behind them opened.

> **[VISUAL:** Slow reveal. Thomas, in a suit, walking in with a cane. Wobbly, but on
> his own two feet. Golden light behind him. Music swells.**]**

**THOMAS** *(slowly, every word an effort)*:
"I'm… not… dead… Vanessa."

> **[VISUAL:** Wide shot. Vanessa drops her purse. Bradley stands up so fast his chair tips.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
It was the first sentence most of them had heard him say in almost a year.

**THOMAS:**
"You moved me… to a closet. You fired… everyone who cared. You tried to… sell my
company… before I was cold."
*(turns to Grace, voice breaking)*
"She… stayed."

> **[VISUAL:** Close-up on Grace, crying, shaking her head. "You don't owe me anything."]**

**NARRATOR:**
Harold read the new terms out loud.

Vanessa's prenup, which she had never bothered to read, meant that divorce left her
with exactly what she'd brought into the marriage. Bradley was removed from the
company and from the will. The power of attorney petition was withdrawn. The police
report was reopened, and this time it named Vanessa for filing a false report.

And Grace got a trust that would pay her for the rest of her life, along with the
deed to a house of her own. The only condition was that she never clean anybody's
floors again unless she wanted to.

**GRACE:**
"Mr. Caldwell, I didn't stay for money."

**THOMAS** *(smiling, the first real smile in a year)*:
"I know. That's… why."

---

### EPILOGUE (14:00–15:00)
> **[VISUAL:** Spring. A small white house with a porch. Grace on a rocking chair.
> Thomas pulls up in a car, slowly climbs the steps with a cane, holding two coffees.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
A year later, Thomas Caldwell was walking a mile a day. He sold one of his mansions
and used the money to start the Caldwell Caregiver Fund, which pays home health aides
across Tennessee a fair wage.

Every Sunday he drives to Grace's little house with two cups of coffee, one of them
black with one ice cube. They sit on the porch and listen to Johnny Cash.

> **[VISUAL:** Two coffee cups on the porch rail. Slow pull-out.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
As for Vanessa and Bradley, the last anyone heard, they were both looking for jobs.

---

### MORAL + CTA (15:00–15:20)
> **[VISUAL:** Black background, white text fading in.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
People show you who they are when you have nothing left to give them. Grace stayed
when everyone else left, and she didn't do it for a reward.

If this story touched you, hit like, subscribe, and tell us in the comments: who's the
person who stayed for you? We read every single one.
