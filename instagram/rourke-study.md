## 2. The cover formula (why the grid looks so strong)

Almost every post is a **Reel** with a custom cover. Each cover follows the same five rules:

1. **He is the main character.** His face is in about 90% of covers, inside a cinematic, AI-generated scene. The scene acts out the tool or feature:
   - *LumaLabs*: he's at a casino table in a tux.
   - *CCTV*: he's running from a dog in a security-camera shot.
   - *Reframe*: he's floating on balloons.
   - *Camera Angles*: he's shot from inside a hole looking up.
   - *Kling 4.0*: he's in a suit on a trading floor.
   - *Rotoscoping*: he's a football player.
2. **Exaggerated emotion.** Shock, screaming, pointing, holding a glowing object. The face is the hook.
3. **A signature prop.** The same cream "Vans" cap in nearly every post. You recognise him at thumbnail size before reading anything.
4. **One giant word in neon yellow.** The tool name (e.g. *Higgsfield*, *Seedance 2.5*, *Magnific*) in a heavy rounded sans-serif with a slight glow. A second line names the feature (*Brand Kit*, *Omni*, *Voices*).
5. **The echo stack.** The feature word repeats 2–3 times underneath, each copy fainter, like a motion trail. Text sometimes sits *behind* his head for a depth effect.

The background changes completely every post: colour, location, genre. The **yellow text, his face and the cap** stay the same. That contrast is what makes the grid feel branded but never repetitive.

## 4. Applying it to @finaltoucch (without copying him)

Keep the formula and change the signature pieces, so the page reads as **yours** rather than a Rourke clone.

| Rourke | @finaltoucch |
|---|---|
| Vans cap | **Your black thick-frame glasses.** They stay in every post. Clothes change with each scene (stylish outfits; see the wardrobe guide in `thumbnail-playbook.md`). |
| Neon yellow text | **Electric lime `#C6FF00`.** Already your brand colour, close enough to pop and different enough to own. |
| "Create 100,000 jobs for creatives" | Suggested mission: **"Helping African creatives earn in dollars with AI."** It ties AI video to Upwork, Contra and remote work, which is a niche he doesn't own. |
| GenHQ community | Later: your own community, course or service, once the audience exists |
| Tool news + tutorials | Same, plus your extra pillars: **design, Upwork/Contra, faceless YouTube** |

### Your cover recipe

1. Generate a cinematic scene with **you** (Higgsfield element `okoye`) acting out the topic. Use a strong expression and keep the glasses.
2. Add the tool or topic name in huge lime text at the top or bottom.
3. Add the feature word with the echo stack (3 copies, fading).
4. Optionally put the text behind your head for depth (cut-out layer).
5. Use 4:5 framing for the grid, with the Reel exported 9:16.

### Example cover ideas

| Topic | Scene with you in it | Text |
|---|---|---|
| Upwork proposals | You at a desk buried under paper letters, shocked | **Upwork** / *Proposals* ×3 |
| Contra portfolio | You in a gallery, your designs framed on the walls | **Contra** / *Portfolio* ×3 |
| Higgsfield camera moves | You mid-air, camera orbiting you on a rooftop at sunset | **Higgsfield** / *Camera* ×3 |
| Faceless YouTube | You as a hooded storyteller by a campfire in an ancient ruin | **Faceless** / *Stories* ×3 |
| Design fixes | You holding a giant glowing poster, before/after split | **Design** / *Fixes* ×3 |
| Remote work | You working on a laptop on a Lagos rooftop at golden hour | **Remote** / *Work* ×3 |

### Posting rhythm

Rourke grew on daily posting. A realistic start for you is **5 Reels a week** (the plan in `README.md`), alternating:
- **AI video:** cinematic clips of you
- **UGC style:** you talking to camera, generated or filmed
- **Static:** carousels and single-image posts

Move toward daily posting once production is smooth.
