## The floor, and why it is a lie

460 s × 6.5 = **2,990 credits** if every shot works first take.

Nothing has worked first take. The record is **883 seconds generated to deliver 275
seconds** — 3.2 seconds shot per second kept, about **15 credits per finished second**.
Any estimate that ignores the retake rate is not an estimate.

## The retake rate is not uniform — this is the whole finding

Split the history by shot type and it separates cleanly:

| Shot type | Examples | Takes per keeper |
|---|---|---|
| **Dialogue / character coverage** | radio scene (4→3), riverbank (6→4), boatyard (6→4) | **~1.5×** |
| **Creature or unfamiliar phenomenon on screen** | shed-skins scale (2 attempts, both failed), ridge of water (2 attempts, both failed, cut), flooded crossing (12 attempts, 1 usable) | **3× to 12×** |

People talking are cheap and predictable. The model has seen a million of them.
The money burns on shots where something the model has never seen must appear — it
substitutes the familiar: the swell became a motorboat, the swell became bioluminescence,
the giant skins came out the same size as the small ones.

## The two costings

**Remaining 460 s splits 240 safe / 220 risky.**

| **As written** — creature shots attempted as scripted | 240 × 6.5 × 1.5 = **2,340** | 220 × 6.5 × 3.0 = **4,290** | **≈ 6,630** |
| **Disciplined** — she is never shown, per the film's own rule | 240 × 6.5 × 1.5 = **2,340** | 220 × 6.5 × 1.5 = **2,145** | **≈ 4,485** |

Cross-check on the as-written figure: 460 s × the historical 15 credits/finished-second
= **6,900**. Two independent methods agree, so 6,600–6,900 is the honest as-written number.
If the hard shots fight the way the flooded crossing did, it goes past **9,000**.

## The recommendation

**Shoot the disciplined version.** It is not only ~2,150 credits cheaper, it is the
better film, and it is the film's own established rule: *the cold open works because she
is never shown.* Every time she has been asked to appear on screen the model has produced
something embarrassing. Every time the beat was carried by a face reacting to something
off-screen, it worked first or second take.

Concretely, for the five risky beats:
- **Shed skins** — drop the "dozens small + two enormous in one frame" reveal. Two shots:
  the wide of the dozens, then one enormous skin with a single small one beside it. The
  edit makes the comparison; the model never has to.
- **Flooded crossing** — the drone lift stays, but she is a *displacement* under an opaque
  surface, not a rendered animal. Faces at water level carry it.
- **Sink cave** — diamond walls catch torchlight. Never the word "glow", or it becomes
  bioluminescent, exactly like the ridge of water did.
- **Hatchlings** — sound, movement at the edge of the torch beam, Kofi's face. Not a
  frame full of small snakes.
- **Deep chamber** — the clutch is eggs, which are static props and render fine. Marcus
  fires *at something off-screen*.

## Correction to an earlier claim

Two days ago the dialogue-last decision was described here as "a large saving". It is
not. Only **two** retired clips failed on audio alone — about **196 credits**. Going
forward it prevents perhaps three to five reshoots, so call it **400–500 credits**. Real,
worth having, not a game-changer. The retake rate on creature shots is the number that
actually decides this budget, and the dub policy does nothing about it.
