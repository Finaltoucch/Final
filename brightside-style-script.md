# Bright Side–Style Video Script

**Working title:** 10 Body Signals You Should Never Ignore
**Alt titles (for A/B thumbnails):**
- "If Your Body Does This, Pay Attention"
- "10 Warning Signs Your Body Is Sending You"
- "Your Body Is Trying to Tell You Something"

**Target length:** ~9 minutes
**Format:** Voiceover + flat 2D animation / stock b-roll, countdown listicle
**Tone:** Upbeat, friendly, curious, second-person ("you")

---

## Channel Analysis (reference)

**Format DNA:** Countdown listicles, single upbeat narrator, no on-camera host,
new visual every 2–4 seconds, big number bumpers, on-screen text echoing key phrases.

**Hook formula (first 15s):** Curiosity gap → personal stakes → tease the best item →
*then* the logo sting.

**Scene rhythm:** Cold-open hook → logo → countdown items (number → claim →
explanation → "aha") → mid-video engagement bait → recap + subscribe/bell CTA.

**Engagement tactics:** Direct questions to comments, tease-ahead numbering,
end-of-video riddle/quiz.

## SCRIPT

### COLD OPEN — HOOK (0:00–0:15)
> **[VISUAL:** Close-up animation of a person rubbing their eye, then a quick
> montage — a hand, a tired face, a glass of water untouched.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Your body talks to you every single day. A twitch here, a strange craving there,
a bruise you don't remember getting. Most of the time you brush it off — but some
of these little signals are your body waving a tiny red flag. And number 3? That
one catches almost everybody off guard.

---

### LOGO STING + PREMISE (0:15–0:28)
> **[VISUAL:** Bright Side logo sting, then a friendly full-body character
> pointing at a checklist.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
So today we're counting down 10 body signals you really shouldn't ignore. Stick
around till the end, because the last one is the easiest to miss. Let's get into it.

---

### #10 — Constantly Cold Hands and Feet (0:28–1:15)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "10" bumper. Character with blue-tinted hands, then a thermometer.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Number 10 — always cold, even when everyone else is comfortable. If your hands and
feet feel like ice cubes no matter the weather, it's usually just poor circulation.
But sometimes it's your body hinting that your thyroid is running a little slow, or
that your iron is low. The fix can be as simple as moving more during the day — or a
quick check-up. Either way, your body's basically asking for a warmer blanket. Noted.

---

### #9 — Cracked Lips That Won't Heal (1:15–2:00)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "9". Animated lips, a water droplet, a banana and spinach.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Number 9 — lips that stay cracked no matter how much balm you use. Chapstick treats
the surface, but if the cracks keep coming back, your body might be short on water or
certain B vitamins. Try drinking a bit more, and add foods like leafy greens or
bananas. Sometimes the smallest signal points to the simplest fix.

---

### #8 — You Bruise Way Too Easily (2:00–2:50)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "8". A gentle bump on a table, a purple bruise blooms in animation.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Number 8 — mystery bruises. You look down and there's a purple mark, and you have no
idea where it came from. A few of these are totally normal. But if they show up
constantly from the tiniest bumps, it can mean your body is low on vitamin C or vitamin K,
the nutrients that help you heal. Keep an eye on how often it happens — patterns matter
more than any single bruise.

---

### #7 — Sudden Sugar or Salt Cravings (2:50–3:40)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "7". A craving cloud with donuts and fries swirling around a head.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Number 7 — cravings that hit out of nowhere. We all want a snack sometimes, but intense,
specific cravings can be your body sending a request. Craving salt might mean you're
dehydrated. Craving sugar can mean your energy is crashing and your body wants a fast
fix. The trick is to listen to the *message*, not just the craving — a glass of water
and a real meal usually do more than the snack ever could.

---

### MID-VIDEO ENGAGEMENT BAIT (3:40–3:55)
> **[VISUAL:** Comment bubble animation, big "👇" pointing down.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Quick question before we keep going — which of these have YOU noticed lately? Drop the
number in the comments, I read them. Okay, back to the countdown, and it's about to get
interesting.

---

### #6 — Your Eye Keeps Twitching (3:55–4:40)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "6". A single twitching eye, a coffee cup, a sleepy moon.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Number 6 — that annoying little eye twitch. It feels dramatic, but it's almost always
harmless. The usual suspects? Too little sleep, too much caffeine, or too much screen
time. Your eye muscle is basically overtired and firing on its own. Rest, hydrate, and
cut back on that fourth coffee — and it usually settles right down.

---

### #5 — Feeling Tired After a Full Night's Sleep (4:40–5:30)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "5". Character waking up already yawning, question marks overhead.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Number 5 — sleeping eight hours and *still* feeling wiped out. If you're getting enough
sleep but waking up exhausted, the issue might not be the quantity — it's the quality.
Snoring, an irregular schedule, or a room that's too warm can all keep you from reaching
deep sleep. Cool, dark, and consistent is the secret combo your body's been asking for.

---

### #4 — Brittle or Ridged Nails (5:30–6:15)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "4". A close-up hand, nails with vertical ridges.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Number 4 — nails that peel, crack, or show deep ridges. Your nails are like a little
status report for the rest of your body. Weak, brittle nails can hint that you're low
on protein, iron, or biotin. It's rarely serious — but it's a nudge to check that your
meals are actually feeding you, not just filling you up.

---

### #3 — You're Always Thirsty (6:15–7:10)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "3" with a spotlight effect. A person gulping water, still thirsty.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Number 3 — and this is the one that surprises people. Being thirsty *all the time*, even
right after drinking, isn't something to shrug off. Yes, it can just mean you need more
water. But constant, unquenchable thirst — especially with frequent trips to the bathroom
— is one of the classic early signals your body sends when your blood sugar is running high.
It's the kind of thing worth mentioning at your next check-up. Not to scare you — just so
you're listening when your body speaks up.

---

### #2 — Unexplained Hair Loss (7:10–7:55)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "2". A hairbrush with a few strands, a calendar flipping pages.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Number 2 — finding more hair on your brush than usual. Shedding some hair every day is
completely normal. But a sudden increase can trace back to stress, a shift in your diet,
or low iron. The good news? Hair loss from these causes is often temporary. Lower the
stress, feed your body well, and give it time to bounce back.

---

### #1 — You Ignore Your Body's "Full" Signal (7:55–8:40)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "1". A clock, a plate, a person eating slowly and pausing.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
And number 1 — the easiest signal to miss: ignoring the moment your body says "I'm full."
Your stomach sends that message about twenty minutes late, so if you eat fast, you blow
right past it. Slowing down, putting the fork down between bites, and actually noticing
that signal is one of the simplest healthy habits there is. Your body's been telling you
all along — the trick is just to listen.

---

### RECAP + CTA OUTRO (8:40–9:00)
> **[VISUAL:** Fast montage of all 10 icons, then the character waving, subscribe
> button + bell animation.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
So there you go — 10 quiet little signals your body sends every day, and what they might
be trying to say. None of this replaces a doctor, but paying attention never hurts.

Now here's a riddle to leave you on: I have no body, but I come alive when you're tired,
and I disappear the moment you rest. What am I? Drop your guess in the comments.

If you learned something new, give this video a like, hit subscribe, and tap that bell so
you never miss the next one. Thanks for watching — and take care of that amazing body of yours.

## Production Notes
- **Pacing:** Swap visuals every 2–4 seconds; never let the screen sit still.
- **On-screen text:** Echo each item's key phrase (e.g. "ALWAYS COLD?") as the narrator says it.
- **Number bumpers:** Big animated countdown number between each item — this drives retention.
- **Music:** Upbeat, low-key royalty-free bed under the whole video.
- **Thumbnail:** Bold face + big number "10" + a red circle/arrow on a body part; high-contrast text.
- **Retention hook:** The "#3 surprises everybody" tease in the intro is what keeps viewers to the mid-point.
- **Compliance:** Keep health claims soft ("might," "can hint at," "worth a check-up") — never diagnostic.
