## THE CAMERA LAW — THIS IS THE WHOLE PROJECT
=====================================================================
The car is LEFT-HAND DRIVE. Whether the driver and steering wheel appear on
the LEFT OF THE PICTURE depends ONLY on where the camera is. It is a mirror.
No prompt wording changes it.

| Camera position             | Driver + wheel appear in frame |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Front, or front three-quarter | viewer's RIGHT  -- FORBIDDEN  |
| Directly behind, receding     | viewer's LEFT   -- allowed    |
| Car's LEFT flank, side-on     | near side, LEFT -- allowed    |
| Car's RIGHT flank             | far side        -- forbidden  |

**NEVER POINT THE CAMERA AT THE FRONT OF THE CAR. NOT ONCE.**
Every shot is filmed from the car's LEFT FLANK or from BEHIND it, or contains
no car at all. If a shot idea needs a front view, the shot idea is wrong.

### DIRECTION LOCK (applies to every shot, never varies)
- The car travels from RIGHT of frame toward LEFT of frame.
- Therefore "ahead" / "forward" = SCREEN LEFT.
- The camera is always on the car's left flank (the pavement side) or behind it.
- The woman stands on the pavement on that same left side.
- When he looks forward, write: "his eyes go out over the low windscreen
  toward the LEFT EDGE OF THE FRAME."

### ANTI-DISTORTION LAW
A large rigid car moving sideways across frame ripples and melts. Fix:
"THE CAMERA IS LOCKED TO THE CAR AND TRAVELS WITH IT AT EXACTLY THE SAME
SPEED. The car STAYS COMPLETELY STILL IN THE FRAME. ALL OF THE MOVEMENT IS
IN THE BACKGROUND." Use this on every moving-car shot.

## PROMPT-WRITING RULES THAT WERE LEARNED THE EXPENSIVE WAY
=====================================================================
1. Paste the FULL car / man / woman spec VERBATIM into every prompt that shows
   that subject. Reference sheets do NOT carry properties between shots. Only
   the prompt text does. Shortening the car spec to "the ivory roadster"
   produced black interiors.
2. Never contradict yourself in one prompt. Banning "window frames" while asking
   for a "chrome windscreen frame" produced a pillar beside his head.
3. State direction against the WORLD and against the FRAME. "Looking ahead" is
   meaningless. "Eyes toward the LEFT EDGE OF THE FRAME" is not.
4. State a PATH, not just a position. "She stands on the pavement" plus "the car
   comes toward camera" made the car bear down on her.
5. Fewer references = more weight each. Two references (man + car) beat three.
6. Avoid the words collision, near miss, impact, jam, hit. They trip the safety
   filter and the job FAILS on the backend.
7. Name who is driving and rule out the alternatives: "he is NOT blonde, NOT
   fair-haired, NOT long-haired, NOT a woman."
