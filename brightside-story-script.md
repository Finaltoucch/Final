# Bright Side–Style Video Script #3 (Animated Story Genre)

**Working title:** Someone Left a Note on My Door Every Morning for a Year
**Alt titles (A/B thumbnails):**
- "I Found a Note on My Door Every Day — Then One Morning It Stopped"
- "My Secret Note-Leaver Turned Out to Be the Last Person I Expected"
- "365 Notes From a Stranger Changed My Life"

**Target length:** ~8 minutes
**Format:** First-person narrated animated story (2D characters, lip-flap animation),
single narrator voicing "I", light dialogue reads for other characters
**Tone:** Cozy-mysterious → tense → heartwarming. Clean, family-friendly, advertiser-safe.

---

## Why this genre

The animated first-person story is the most-watched format on Bright Side–style
channels right now. It retains differently from listicles and "What If" videos:
instead of numbers or time stages, it runs on **an unanswered question** ("who is
doing this?"), **escalating suspicion**, a **break in the pattern**, and a
**reveal that reframes everything** — then lands an emotional moral viewers want
to comment about.

**Story beat map:**
Hook (the mystery + the pattern break) → normal life → strange event →
escalation → failed investigation → the pattern breaks → reveal →
payoff/role reversal → moral → comment question + CTA.

## SCRIPT

### COLD OPEN — HOOK (0:00–0:18)
> **[VISUAL:** Close-up of a yellow sticky note on an apartment door: *"Take an
> umbrella today."* Quick cuts to a wall covered in hundreds of identical yellow
> notes. Then: an empty door. No note. Ominous music sting.**]**

**NARRATOR (Mia):**
For one whole year, I found a yellow sticky note on my front door every single
morning. Three hundred and sixty-four of them. I never once saw who left them.
Then, on day three hundred and sixty-five… there was no note. And that empty door
turned out to be the most important message of all.

---

### LOGO STING + SETUP (0:18–0:35)
> **[VISUAL:** Logo sting. Mia, early twenties, dragging boxes up a narrow
> staircase into a small, old apartment building.**]**

**NARRATOR (Mia):**
My name's Mia. Last year I moved to a new city for my first real job. I didn't
know a single person there. My building was old, quiet, and full of neighbors I
only knew by the sound of their doors closing.

---

### ACT 1 — THE FIRST NOTE (0:35–1:35)
> **[VISUAL:** Mia opening her door, half-asleep, holding coffee. A yellow note
> flutters. On-screen text echoes it.**]**

**NARRATOR (Mia):**
On my third morning, I opened the door and there it was. One yellow sticky note,
neat handwriting, all capital letters:

**ON-SCREEN NOTE:** *"TAKE AN UMBRELLA TODAY."*

The sky was perfectly blue. I laughed, stuck it on my fridge, and went to work
without an umbrella.

> **[VISUAL:** Mia at a bus stop. Sudden cartoon downpour. She's soaked; a
> pigeon next to her is somehow dry.**]**

By noon, I looked like I'd walked through a car wash. Okay. Lucky guess.

---

### ACT 2 — THE NOTES KEEP COMING (1:35–2:45)
> **[VISUAL:** Fast montage of mornings — calendar pages flipping, a new note each
> time, Mia's expression shifting from amused to puzzled.**]**

**NARRATOR (Mia):**
Except it wasn't one note. The next morning there was another. And the next.

**ON-SCREEN NOTES (rapid):**
*"YOUR BACK LEFT TIRE IS LOW."*
*"BUS 14 IS DETOURED TODAY. TAKE 22."*
*"YOU LEFT YOUR KITCHEN LIGHT ON AGAIN."*

And every single one was right. My tire *was* low. Bus 14 *was* detoured. And
yes — I'd been leaving my kitchen light on every night.

Which raised a pretty uncomfortable question: how did this person know my
kitchen light was on?

---

### ACT 3 — SUSPICION (2:45–3:40)
> **[VISUAL:** Mia peeking through the peephole. Cartoon "suspect board" with
> neighbor portraits and red string.**]**

**NARRATOR (Mia):**
So I did what anyone would do. I made a suspect list.

There was Derek downstairs, who played drums at 11 p.m. and definitely wasn't
awake at sunrise. The couple next door, who were barely ever home. And across the
hall, Mr. Halvorsen — a quiet older man in a gray cardigan who'd never said more
than a nod to me.

I was a little creeped out. Kind of curious. And honestly? Kind of grateful,
because those notes were saving me every week.

Quick question for you — would you be creeped out, or grateful? Tell me in the
comments. I genuinely couldn't decide.

---

### ACT 4 — THE STAKEOUT THAT FAILED (3:40–4:35)
> **[VISUAL:** Mia on the floor by her door with snacks and a blanket, eyes
> drooping. Clock reads 2:00… 3:30… 4:45 a.m. She falls asleep mid-chip.**]**

**NARRATOR (Mia):**
One night I decided to catch them. I sat by my door with snacks, a flashlight,
and way too much confidence. I lasted until 4:45 a.m.

I woke up to sunlight — and a note stuck to the *outside* of the door I'd been
sleeping against.

**ON-SCREEN NOTE:** *"THE FLOOR IS BAD FOR YOUR BACK. GO TO BED."*

> **[VISUAL:** Mia staring at the note, mouth open, comedic freeze-frame.**]**

So… they knew I was there. I decided right then to stop investigating. Whoever
it was clearly wasn't trying to scare me. They were looking out for me. I started
keeping every note in a shoebox.

---

### MID-VIDEO TEASE (4:35–4:50)
> **[VISUAL:** Shoebox overflowing with yellow notes. Calendar flips to day 364.**]**

**NARRATOR (Mia):**
Months went by. Notes about snowstorms, sales at the grocery store, and once,
just: *"YOU LOOK TIRED. IT'S OKAY TO REST."*
And then came day three hundred and sixty-five.

---

### ACT 5 — THE PATTERN BREAKS (4:50–5:50)
> **[VISUAL:** Mia opens her door, smiling, reaching for a note — but the door is
> bare. Music drops out. Close-up on the empty spot.**]**

**NARRATOR (Mia):**
I opened the door. No note.

It sounds silly, but my stomach dropped. A whole year, rain or shine, holidays
included — never missed once. I stood there in the hallway, and for the first
time I really looked at the door across from mine.

Mr. Halvorsen's door. His newspaper was still on the mat. And Mr. Halvorsen
*always* picked up his paper by six.

> **[VISUAL:** Mia knocking. No answer. A faint sound from inside.**]**

I knocked. Nothing. Then I heard something very quiet from inside — like
someone trying to call out but not having the strength.

---

### ACT 6 — THE REVEAL (5:50–6:50)
> **[VISUAL:** Building manager unlocking the door. Mr. Halvorsen sitting on the
> floor by his window, conscious but unable to stand. Mia kneeling beside him,
> phone to her ear. Gentle, non-graphic framing.**]**

**NARRATOR (Mia):**
I ran for the building manager. We got the door open, and there was
Mr. Halvorsen — he'd slipped the night before and couldn't get back up. He was
okay, but he'd been there for hours. I called for help and held his hand until
they arrived.

And that's when I saw it.

> **[VISUAL:** Slow pan across his windowsill: a weather radio, a pair of
> binoculars pointed at the parking lot, a bus schedule with routes circled, and
> a pad of yellow sticky notes.**]**

His window looked straight down at the parking lot — and right across the
courtyard into my kitchen. Next to his chair sat a weather radio, a bus schedule
with routes circled in pen, and a pad of yellow sticky notes.

**MR. HALVORSEN (weakly, embarrassed):**
"I'm sorry I missed today."

---

### ACT 7 — THE WHY (6:50–7:30)
> **[VISUAL:** Hospital visit a few days later. Mr. Halvorsen in bed, Mia beside
> him with the shoebox. A faded framed photo of a young woman in his hand.**]**

**NARRATOR (Mia):**
A few days later, I visited him and finally got the story. He'd lost his wife
years ago, and he barely slept anymore. He'd sit by the window every night with
the radio on. When I moved in, he said I reminded him of his daughter, who lives
overseas — always rushing, always forgetting her umbrella.

He didn't know how to just knock and say hello. So he wrote it down instead.

**MR. HALVORSEN:**
"It gave me a reason to wake up early."

---

### PAYOFF — ROLE REVERSAL (7:30–7:50)
> **[VISUAL:** Mia, pen in hand, sticking a yellow note on HIS door. Close-up of
> the note. Mr. Halvorsen opening his door, reading it, smiling.**]**

**NARRATOR (Mia):**
So now, every morning, *I* leave a note on *his* door.

**ON-SCREEN NOTE:** *"BREAKFAST AT 8. I'M BRINGING THE COFFEE. — YOUR NEIGHBOR."*

And every morning, he's up and waiting.

---

### MORAL + COMMENT QUESTION + CTA (7:50–8:10)
> **[VISUAL:** Wide shot of the building at sunrise, two windows lit side by
> side. Subscribe button + bell animation.**]**

**NARRATOR (Mia):**
Sometimes the people looking out for us are the ones we never think to look in
on. If there's a neighbor you only know by the sound of their door — maybe
knock today.

Have you ever had a stranger quietly look out for you? Share your story in the
comments — I read every one.

And if this story made you smile, hit like, subscribe, and ring the bell for more
stories like this. See you next time — and don't forget your umbrella.

## Production Notes
- **Hook rule:** The cold open shows the *pattern* (notes wall) and the *pattern
  break* (empty door) before the logo — that unanswered "why did it stop?" carries
  viewers through the slower setup.
- **Recurring visual motif:** The yellow sticky note is the brand of this video —
  thumbnail, transitions, and on-screen text should all use the same yellow.
  Use a note "peel" as the scene transition between acts.
- **Plant and pay off:** Everything in the reveal must be seeded earlier — the
  kitchen light (window view), the bus detour (bus schedule), the umbrella (weather
  radio), the gray cardigan, the 6 a.m. newspaper. Viewers love rewatching to spot clues.
- **Comedy beats:** Keep 2–3 light gags (dry pigeon, falling asleep mid-chip,
  the "GO TO BED" note) in Acts 1–4 so the tonal drop in Act 5 hits harder.
- **Tone guardrail:** The fall is handled gently and off-screen — no injury
  visuals, no medical detail. He's "okay," help arrives, and the story moves to warmth.
  Keep the Act 3 "creepy" angle playful, never threatening.
- **Disclaimer card (end screen or description):** "This story is a dramatization.
  Names and details are fictional." — standard practice for this format.
- **Thumbnail:** Split image — Mia's shocked face on the left, an empty door with
  a single yellow note-shaped outline on the right; text: "IT STOPPED".
- **Music:** Light pizzicato for Acts 1–4, silence at the empty door, soft piano
  from the reveal onward, warm swell at the role reversal.
- **Engagement:** Two comment prompts — the "creeped out or grateful?" debate
  question mid-video (drives replies between viewers) and the "share your story"
  prompt at the end (drives long comments, which the algorithm rewards).
