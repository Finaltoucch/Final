# THE HOUR CUT — SIX DEATHS, EACH A SET PIECE

Finaltouch: *"i need a full hour of action scenes showing their death one by one."*

This is a different film from the 12-minute cut. Not a longer version of it — a different
build, where each death is a sequence rather than a beat.

## WHAT MAKES EACH DEATH DIFFERENT

An hour of deaths fails if they are six versions of the same thing. Each is built on a
different mechanism:

| | Death | The mechanism | Who is to blame |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mateo | **Taken while held** — they have hands on him and still lose | Nobody. It is simply stronger |
| Liam | **Taken upward** — the only vertical attack, from the canopy | His own curiosity. He flew the drone |
| Daniel | **No contact at all** — fear stops his heart | Himself. He hid a diamond |
| Kofi | **Chosen** — he turns and kneels | Ryan, for pushing on |
| Maya | **Chosen** — she takes the handle out of Ryan's hand | Ryan, and she says so |
| Marcus | **Chosen** — he steps into open ground | Ryan, and Marcus tells him to go anyway |

The first three are done *to* them. The last three are chosen — and all three are chosen
for Ryan. That is the film's argument, and it only lands if the deaths are not
interchangeable.

## THE HONEST WARNING

At 7 minutes a sequence, each death is roughly **14 clips at 30 seconds**. On this
production's record, expect to shoot **20–25** to keep 14. That is where the gap between
23,400 and 32,000 comes from, and it is not padding.

The rules now written into `SHOT-CHECKLIST.md` exist to shrink that gap — the element
mechanism, the three-character cap, rough uncut diamonds, no size in prose, no
absence-phrasing. Every one of them was learned by wasting credits. They should make the
hour cheaper per usable second than the twelve minutes were.
