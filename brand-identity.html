Ten minutes. One “ohhh.”

The identity for a voiceover-and-animation channel that makes countdown listicles about your body and cinematic “What if…” scenarios. Every video is built toward the moment the idea clicks, and the brand is built around that moment too.

01 · Name

Why “Penny Drop”

“The penny drops” is the English idiom for the instant someone finally understands. Your scripts already end every segment on an “aha” beat, so the name describes the product. It also gives you a sound (a coin clink), a shape (a circle) and a color (copper) for free.

Recommended Penny Drop

Idiom everyone knows, ownable sound and mascot, works as a verb in copy (“wait for the drop”).

Huh Factory

Playful and memorable. Slightly jokey for body-health topics where you need trust.

Wonderloop

Suits “What if…” videos well. Says less about the payoff and is harder to turn into a sound or icon.

Second Look

Fits the “signals you ignore” angle. Feels calm and adult; weaker for the cinematic scenarios.

Before committing, check the handle on YouTube, TikTok and Instagram and run a trademark search in your country.

02 · Positioning

What the channel stands for

For people who scroll for something interesting, Penny Drop turns everyday mysteries into ten-minute stories that end with the answer clicking.

Audience

Curious 16–34 year olds watching on phones and TVs. They want something light but real: facts they can repeat at dinner.

Promise

Every video pays off its title. If the thumbnail asks a question, the video answers it clearly before the end screen.

Difference

Big listicle channels go wide and fast. Penny Drop slows down for one clear “why” per item, and says when science is unsure.

FORMAT · COUNTDOWN 10→1

Body Signals

Health listicles like “10 Body Signals You Should Never Ignore.” Friendly, never alarming; always points to a doctor for real symptoms.

FORMAT · TIME-STAGE ESCALATION

What If…

Cinematic scenarios like “What If the Earth Stopped Spinning for 5 Seconds?” told second by second with a twist in the last act.

FORMAT · COUNTDOWN OR STORY

Everyday Science

A third pillar to test: why ordinary things work the way they do (fridges, yawns, airplane windows).

03 · Logo

The coin mark

A copper coin with a single-stroke P. The motion version drops three speed lines and lands on a slot, timed to the clink in the sound logo. Use the coin alone at small sizes and the coin with wordmark everywhere else.

Primary lockup on white

Reversed on Deep Teal

Profile picture (800 × 800, circle-safe)

Clear space

  • Keep empty space around the logo equal to half the coin’s diameter.
  • Minimum size: coin 24 px on screen, wordmark 120 px wide.

Don’t

  • Recolor the coin anything but copper.
  • Add a drop shadow, bevel or metallic gradient.
  • Stack the wordmark on Mint; contrast is too low.

Sound logo

  • A single bright coin clink, then a soft “whoomp” bass hit. 0.8 s total.
  • Plays exactly once per video, after the cold-open hook.

06 · Voice

How Penny Drop sounds

One narrator, talking straight to “you.” The voice is a smart friend leaning in, not a presenter on a stage.

CuriousWarmClear before cleverSecond personHonest about uncertainty

Say

“Your body talks to you every single day.”
“Here’s the part nobody tells you.”
“Scientists aren’t sure yet, but the best guess is…”
“If this happens a lot, it’s worth a quick chat with your doctor.”

Don’t say

“This one simple trick doctors hate!”
“You won’t BELIEVE number 3!!!”
“This is 100% proven.”
“If you have this, you’re in danger.”
