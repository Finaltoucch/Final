# THE MONEY

**A five-minute drama · love and betrayal · Ben & Gabby**

25 shots × 12 seconds = 300 seconds
16:9 · Cinema Studio · pro · silent + voiceover

---

## The Design

**The hook gives away the ending in the first twelve seconds**, then spends five
minutes making you watch it happen anyway. Nobody clicks away from a question
they've already been handed the answer to — they stay to find out *how*.

**The argument is built in.** Viewers will split three ways, and none of the three
is obviously wrong:

- *She had no right to go through his things.*
- *He had no right to keep it from her.*
- *Her mother is the one who did the real damage.*

There is no villain. That's the point, and that's what fills a comment section.

**VO:** *She was certain. She was also wrong. It took eleven months to find that out.*

**VO:** *Four years. Everybody said they'd make it.*

**VO:** *It wasn't a dramatic love. It was a Tuesday kind of love.*

### 4

**VO:** *He'd tell people he got lucky. He meant it.*

### 5

**VO:** *The first time, it was nothing.*

**VISUAL:** Gabby notices the face-down phone. Something passes across her face.

**VO:** *She decided it was nothing. That's a decision, not a fact.*

**VISUAL:** Supermarket checkout. His card declines. He laughs it off, pays with

**VO:** *He said work was slow. Work was not slow.*

### 8
**VISUAL:** Bedroom, dark. She asks him something. He says *"I've got it

**VO:** *Four words. He'd say them a hundred times that year.*

## ACT TWO — THE DISCOVERY

### 9
**VISUAL:** His phone lights up on the arm of the sofa. A banking alert.
**VISIBLE TEXT:** `Transfer sent — £850`
**VO:** *Eight hundred and fifty pounds. Every month. For two years.*

### 10
**VISUAL:** Gabby, very still, staring at the screen. Not touching it yet.
**VO:** *She hadn't looked. Not yet. That mattered to her, later.*

### 11
**VISUAL:** She asks him directly across the kitchen table. He doesn't lie — he
just says nothing, and looks away.
**VO:** *He could have ended it here with one sentence. He'd made a promise not to.*

### 12
**VISUAL:** Empty flat. She stands in front of his desk drawer for a long moment.
Then opens it.
**VO:** *This is the part she never defends.*

### 13
**VISUAL:** Bank statements, folded. The same transfer, over and over. A name she
doesn't recognise.
**VISIBLE TEXT:** `R. ADEYEMI`
**VO:** *A name. Twenty-four payments. No explanation anywhere in the flat.*

### 14
**VISUAL:** Under the papers — a photograph. A woman in her fifties, and a young
child, at a garden table.
**VO:** *And a photograph he'd never shown her.*

### 15
**VISUAL:** Confrontation. She holds up the photo. He goes white. He says
*"I can't tell you."* He means it as mercy. It doesn't land as mercy.
**VO:** *Three words. He thought they were kindness.*

### 16
**VISUAL:** Gabby at a café with a friend, talking, crying, the photo on the
table between them.
**VO:** *By the weekend, everyone they knew had a version of it.*

### 17 · RETURN TO SHOT 1
**VISUAL:** The suitcase. The doorway. Ben pleading. She walks past him.
**VO:** *He didn't stop her. She's never forgiven him for that either.*

## ACT THREE — THE COST

### 18
**VISUAL:** Eleven months later. Gabby alone in a new flat, smaller. Sitting on
the floor eating from a takeaway box.
**VO:** *She was fine. She told people she was fine.*

### 19
**VISUAL:** A letter arrives. Care-home letterhead. She reads it standing in the
hallway, coat still on.
**VO:** *The letter was about her mother's account. It was overdue.*

### 20
**VISUAL:** Care home corridor. Gabby walking, quick, coat flapping.
**VO:** *Overdue for the first time in twenty-six months.*

### 21
**VISUAL:** A room. Her mother in a chair by the window. Frail, lucid, pleased to
see her.
**VO:** *Eight hundred and fifty pounds a month. Room fourteen.*

### 22
**VISUAL:** A name plate at the nurses' station.
**VISIBLE TEXT:** `R. ADEYEMI — CARE MANAGER`
And on the desk, the same photograph — the manager, and her grandchild.
**VO:** *The woman in the photograph ran the home. The child was her granddaughter.*

### 23
**VISUAL:** Her mother, taking Gabby's hand, explaining. Gabby's face going
slowly to nothing.
**VO:** *Her mother said: he made me promise. He said you'd never take it if you knew.*

### 24
**VISUAL:** Gabby outside the care home, back against the wall, sliding down it.
Phone in her hand. Ben's name on the screen. She doesn't press call.
**VO:** *She's had eleven months to think of what to say. She still hasn't called.*

### 25 · CLOSE
**VISUAL:** The same restaurant, same table, from shot 5. Ben alone. Two glasses,
one untouched. His phone face down. He turns it over — nothing.
**VO:** *He kept his promise. That's what it cost him.*
**HARD CUT TO BLACK.**

## The Argument

The comment section will fight about this, which is the point:

| Position | The case |
|---|---|
| **She was justified** | Two years, £20,400, a hidden photograph, and he refused to explain. Anyone would have looked. |
| **She destroyed it** | She went through his drawer, then told everyone they knew, on a story she'd invented. |
| **He was the coward** | "I can't tell you" is not honesty. He chose a promise to her mother over the woman he lived with. |
| **Her mother caused it** | She extracted a promise that made a lie inevitable, then let her daughter's relationship die inside it. |

**Every one of those is defensible.** No villain, no clean answer — so people argue
instead of agreeing.

---

## Pinned comment

> He kept a promise he made to her mother. She looked in a drawer she'd been told
> not to open. Who broke it first?

That single question is what turns a view into a comment.

## The Numbers That Hold It Together

Keep these exact and consistent — they're what make it feel true rather than
written:

| | |
|---|---|
| £850 | monthly transfer |
| 24 → 26 | payments (statement shows 24; mother has been there 26 months) |
| 11 months | separation before she finds out |
| 4 years | together |
| Room 14 | her mother's room |
| R. Adeyemi | care manager |
