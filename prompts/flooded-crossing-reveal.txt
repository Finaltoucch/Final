DIAMOND ANACONDA — THE FLOODED CROSSING. One continuous 30-second shot, no cuts.

Photorealistic live-action cinema, 35mm, fine film grain. Flat green daylight under a
closed jungle canopy, humid, no sun.

LOCATION: a flooded forest. The forest floor is underwater. Brown, slow-moving water runs
between the tree trunks in every direction.

THE CAMERA MOVES IN ONE UNBROKEN TAKE FROM WATER LEVEL TO DIRECTLY OVERHEAD.

SECONDS 0 TO 12 — WATER LEVEL, CLOSE.
The camera sits low, just above the water, close to the group. Six people wade forward
through the flooded forest with the water at their waists. They are spread out in a loose,
uneven, staggered group — some further forward, some further back, none of them level with
each other, the way real people spread out crossing water. They wear olive and khaki
tactical clothing and chest rigs, and carry rifles held up in both hands above the water.
The water is at waist height on every one of them.
They move slowly, because the water is heavy. Their bodies push small bow waves ahead of
them. Insects sit on the surface.
The surface of the water is opaque. It is flat, brown and matte, and it mirrors the green
canopy overhead. Looking across it from this height you see reflected leaves. You do not
see into it.

THE SHOT CONTAINS EXACTLY TWO SPOKEN LINES AND NOTHING ELSE. Speak both slowly and
clearly, with a pause between them. Do not add any other words.

The woman with long dark hair, quietly: "We are the only thing moving out here."
(pause)
The team leader, quietly: "I know."

SECONDS 12 TO 20 — THE CAMERA CLIMBS.
The camera rises steadily and continuously, straight up. It never stops and never comes
back down. It lifts off the water, past the wading figures, past their raised rifles, up
through a gap in the canopy, and keeps climbing. The six figures become smaller and
smaller below, still wading forward in their loose staggered group across a wide sheet of
brown water.

SECONDS 20 TO 30 — DIRECTLY OVERHEAD, LOOKING STRAIGHT DOWN.
The camera is now high above the flooded forest, looking straight down. From directly
overhead the surface no longer mirrors the canopy — the reflection is gone, the water
reads as deep transparent brown, and the shapes under it can be seen.
Beneath the surface, a very long dark snake glides in from the left of frame. She is
enormous, many times longer than the group is wide, and she swims about four feet under
the water on a steady course that carries her straight across their line. Her body is dark
olive and matte, patterned in darker blotches, as thick as a barrel, and she moves in slow
lateral waves from head to tail. She is unhurried.
She crosses directly between two of the wading figures, close beside them, passes under
the group, and continues out through the flooded trees on the same steady course.
The six figures keep wading forward the whole time, rifles up, each one watching their own
direction across the water, continuing steadily toward the far side.

The water stays dark, brown and matte throughout. There are no lights on the snake.
