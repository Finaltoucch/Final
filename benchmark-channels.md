## The Single Most Important Finding

### Company Man — 1.79M subs, 317.9M lifetime views, started 2017

The production quality is **basic**. Voiceover over simple charts, company logos,
and stock photographs. No animation rigs. No motion design. Nothing on screen
that couldn't be built in Canva in fifteen minutes.

**It has 1.79 million subscribers.**

This is the proof that production value is not the moat in this niche. Company
Man wins on topic selection and narrative structure — "why did this company
die" told cleanly, every week, for eight years. The graphics are a delivery
mechanism, not the product.

If you only study one channel, study this one. It is the closest thing to
evidence that our plan works, and it clears the bar for our stack with room to
spare.

**Reality check on money:** third-party estimates put Company Man around
$3,800–5,300/month. Those estimates are unreliable and exclude sponsorships,
which for a channel that size are likely the larger line. But it's a useful
anchor against the source thread's "$9,400 in month eight" — a channel with
1.79M subscribers and eight years of library is in that same range. **The thread's
numbers were extraordinary, not typical.** Plan accordingly.

## What Each One Is Worth Stealing

**Company Man** — the rise/fall narrative arc, and the confidence to keep visuals
plain. Maps directly onto our Block B.

**How Money Works** — pacing and the "systems, not advice" framing. Explains how
money moves without telling you what to do with yours. Exactly our YMYL guardrail,
executed at 1.7M subs.

**Logically Answered** — proof that volume works. High output, simple visuals,
886K subs. This is the model if we go two videos a week.

**Wall Street Millennial** — 364K on genuinely minimal production. Charts and
screen recordings. Shows the floor is lower than we assumed.

**Sime Media** — the visual system, already broken down in
`visual-style-reference.md`. Closest match to our plan in both niche and method.

**Bright Sun Films** — the *format* transfers (dead companies, forensic tone), the
*footage* does not. They shoot real abandoned locations. We'd substitute archival
photos and generated stills, which is a genuine downgrade for that specific
format. Take the topic list, not the approach.

**Economics Explained** — take the topic scope (country and system-level
economics), leave the custom animation.

**PolyMatter and Modern MBA** — study the writing, ignore everything else. These
are studio operations.

## The Warning Sign in This Data

Modern MBA explicitly markets **"every episode is entirely original with no
AI-generated content."**

That's a positioning choice, and it only works as positioning if a meaningful
part of this audience treats AI content as a negative. They do. Business and
finance viewers are unusually allergic to slop, because the whole value
proposition is "the research is real."

**What this means for us, concretely:**

1. **The AI has to be invisible.** Obviously-generated video — morphing hands,
   melting text, uncanny motion — actively costs us credibility in this niche.
   This is another argument for the Sime Media approach: clean graded stills with
   restrained motion read as *design*, not as AI output.
2. **The research has to be genuinely real.** Sourced numbers, real filings. This
   is the one place we cannot cut corners, and it's the thing that makes the
   difference between us and the hundreds of channels running the same stack.
3. **Never advertise how it's made.** The disclosure toggle at upload is required
   and we tick it. Branding the channel around AI is not.

The differentiator isn't the tooling — everyone has the tooling. It's the writing
and the sourcing.

---

## Reproducibility Verdict

**Six of nine benchmark channels are within reach of Claude + Higgsfield + CapCut**,
including three above a million subscribers. The two we can't touch are literal
studios with broadcast staff, and one relies on physical location shooting.

The gap between us and Company Man is not equipment. It's 400 videos and eight
years of practice at picking topics and structuring stories — which is precisely
the part Claude can compress.
