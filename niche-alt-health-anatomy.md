# Alternative Niche — Health, Anatomy & How the Body Works

Evaluated against the business/money explainer recommendation, using the same
constraints: no expertise, own cloned voice, stick figure animation, faceless.

**Date:** 2026-08-02

---

## Why It's Tempting

### Proven ceiling

| Channel | Subs | Format |
|---|---|---|
| **Zack D. Films** | **27.85M** | Faceless animated body/anatomy facts |
| Institute of Human Anatomy | 8.44M | Real cadavers, **on-camera host** — not faceless |

Zack D. Films is the headline: nearly 28 million subscribers on faceless animated
anatomy content. That's more than double the biggest stick figure comedy channel
and ten times any business explainer we found.

### It fits the animation style better than finance does

Anatomy is inherently visual and inherently diagrammatic. Line art of a heart, a
lung, a nerve pathway — in black with one oxblood accent — is *exactly* what the
Recraft vector style produces well. Our finance plan has to invent metaphors
(a car for debt, a bottle for ink). Anatomy comes with its own imagery.

### It removes the US-targeting constraint

This is the real strategic advantage. Our business plan required US-specific
content — US salaries, US tax rules, US brands — because that's where the ad
money is. **Everyone has a body.** Anatomy content travels globally without
rewriting, which massively widens the addressable audience.

### Evergreen and infinite

How the liver works doesn't change. Neither does why you get goosebumps, hiccup,
or bruise. The topic supply is effectively unlimited and the library never
decays.

## The Reason I Still Don't Recommend It First

### The yellow dollar cliff

Health and wellness content earns **$8–20 CPM when it stays "green."** But
YouTube's automated systems routinely flag health, nutrition, mental health, and
body-image content as "not suitable for most advertisers."

**Yellow-dollar CPM falls to $0.50–$2. That's a 50–90% revenue cut.**

It happens algorithmically, before a human sees it, and appealing takes days
during which the video does most of its lifetime views. In business explainers
this failure mode essentially doesn't exist. In health it's a recurring tax.

That single fact is why finance beats health on expected revenue even though
health has the bigger audience.

### Zack D. Films' 28M is Shorts scale, not long-form scale

His dominance is built on **Shorts**, which monetize at a small fraction of
long-form rates. Subscriber count is not comparable to a long-form channel's, and
copying his format means competing in the lowest-paying format on the platform.
Don't read 27.85M as 27.85M worth of revenue.

### Accuracy risk is higher than in business

Getting a company's revenue split wrong is embarrassing. Getting something wrong
about a medication, a symptom, or a treatment can hurt someone, and YouTube's
medical misinformation enforcement is considerably harder than its business
enforcement. With no domain expertise, the margin for error is thinner here than
anywhere else we've looked.

### What changed in January 2026

YouTube did relax advertiser guidelines, restoring full monetization to several
previously restricted categories (self-harm awareness, suicide prevention,
abortion education) when handled non-graphically. Real improvement — but it
addresses the *hardest* categories, not the everyday auto-flagging of ordinary
health and nutrition content. The cliff is still there.

## The Version That Actually Works

If you want this niche, take the same trick that made the business plan safe:
**explain the mechanism, never give the advice.**

| ✅ Do this | ❌ Not this |
|---|---|
| Why you get goosebumps | How to boost your immune system |
| What actually happens when you crack a knuckle | Should you take this supplement |
| Why your stomach growls | How to fix your gut health |
| Why bruises change colour | Signs you have a vitamin deficiency |
| How your body knows when to wake up | Best sleep supplements ranked |
| Why you can't tickle yourself | How to lose belly fat |

The left column is **curiosity about the body**. The right column is **health
advice** — YMYL, flag-prone, and requires expertise neither of us has.

Staying strictly in the left column:
- avoids most auto-flag triggers (no disease, no treatment, no body image, no
  mental health, no nutrition claims)
- requires no credentials, because it's third-person biology
- is far more shareable — "why do we hiccup" beats "immune tips" every time
- is perfectly suited to the stick figure style

That framing turns the niche from risky to viable. It is genuinely a good
channel concept.

## Head to Head

| | Business & money | Body mechanics |
|---|---|---|
| RPM (green) | **$12–22** | $8–20 |
| Yellow-dollar risk | Low | **High — the deciding factor** |
| Audience ceiling | Large | **Enormous** |
| Geographic targeting | US required | **None — global** |
| Fits stick figure style | Good (metaphors needed) | **Excellent (native imagery)** |
| Accuracy risk if wrong | Embarrassing | **Can harm someone** |
| Competition | Medium, crowded at top | High, but thin in long-form |
| Sponsor quality | **Excellent (fintech, B2B)** | Mixed (supplement offers — decline them) |

---

## Recommendation

**Stay with business & money for channel one.** The yellow-dollar cliff is the
whole argument: a 50–90% revenue cut that fires automatically is worse than a
slightly smaller audience.

**But body mechanics is the strongest second channel**, and a legitimate first
choice if you'd rather optimise for reach than for RPM. It fits the animation
style better than finance does, and losing the US-targeting constraint is a real
advantage that shouldn't be dismissed.

**If you switch:** live strictly in the left column above, and never accept a
supplement sponsorship. That's the sector that will come to you first and it's
the one that ends channels.
