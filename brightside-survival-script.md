# Bright Side–Style Video Script #3 (Survival Genre)

**Working title:** How to Survive 8 Animal Encounters Most People Get Wrong
**Alt titles (A/B thumbnails):**
- "If You Ever Meet These Animals, Do THIS"
- "8 Animal Encounters — And the One Move That Saves You"
- "You've Been Told the Wrong Way to Survive These"

**Target length:** ~10 minutes
**Format:** Voiceover + flat 2D animation, countdown listicle with a "myth vs. right move" twist per item
**Tone:** Calm-but-urgent, friendly, second-person — the viewer is always the one in the scenario

---

## Channel Analysis (reference)

**Why this genre:** Bright Side's single most-viewed upload ever is a wild-animal
survival listicle (200M+ views). Survival content wins because every item carries
built-in stakes ("this could be YOU one day"), and each entry has a natural
mini-arc: danger → common mistake → correct move → relief.

**Scene grammar (what's on screen, always):**
- New visual every 2–4 seconds; the screen never sits still.
- Big animated number bumper between items — the retention metronome.
- A recurring everyman character (round, expressive, slightly hapless) who
  *demonstrates the mistake first*, then the correct move — mistake/fix is the
  visual joke engine of the whole channel.
- On-screen text echoes the key command ("DON'T RUN", "BACK AWAY SLOWLY").
- Danger is cartoonish, never graphic — advertiser-safe, kid-adjacent.

**Hook formula (first 15 seconds, before the logo):**
1. Instant scenario — drop the viewer into danger in the second person.
2. Contradiction — "everything you've heard about this is wrong."
3. Tease a specific numbered item ("and wait till you see number 2").

**Retention devices:** tease-ahead numbering, mid-video comment question,
a held-back "most surprising" item, end riddle, subscribe/bell CTA.

## SCRIPT

**NARRATOR:**
Picture this: you're on a hike, you turn a corner — and there's a bear, twenty
feet away, looking right at you. Your legs are already screaming RUN. And that
instinct? That's exactly what could get you hurt. Today, we're fixing the survival
advice you've had wrong your whole life — and number 2 is the one almost everyone
fails.

### LOGO STING + PREMISE (0:16–0:30)
> **[VISUAL:** Bright Side logo sting. The everyman character salutes in a safari
> hat; eight animal silhouettes line up behind him.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Eight animal encounters, eight moves that actually work — straight from what
wildlife experts recommend. Your instincts will fight you on half of these. Let's

---

### #8 — A Dog Charges You (0:30–1:35)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "8" bumper. Character on a sidewalk; a dog sprints at him.
> He runs — red X. Rewind sound. He stands still, side-on, hands in — green check.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Number 8 — a strange dog runs at you, barking. The mistake? Running, screaming, or
waving your arms — to a dog, that's an invitation to chase. Here's the right move:
stop. Stand still like a tree, turn slightly sideways, keep your hands close, and
avoid staring straight into its eyes. No prey signals, no threat signals. Most dogs
lose interest in something that's suddenly... boring. If it does knock you down, curl
up, protect your neck with your hands, and stay still. Boring saves the day. Remember
that — "be a tree" comes back later in this list.

---

### #7 — A Snake Blocks the Path (1:35–2:35)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "7". A coiled snake on a trail; character pokes it with a
> stick — red X. Rewind. He takes two big slow steps back — green check, snake
> slides away.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Number 7 — a snake, right in the middle of your path. Forget everything the movies
taught you: no stick, no rock, no heroic photo. Nearly all snake bites happen when
people try to handle or move the snake. The right move is beautifully simple — stop,
then back away slowly. A snake can strike about half its body length, so two big
steps put you out of the danger zone. It doesn't want you; you're way too big to eat.
Give it space, and it exits the scene on its own. Easiest win on this list.

---

### #6 — Jellyfish Sting (2:35–3:35)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "6". Beach scene. Character gets stung; a friend runs up with
> a comedy "folk remedy" bottle — big red X. Rewind. Seawater rinse, card scraping,
> hot water bucket — green checks.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Number 6 — the jellyfish sting, home of the most famous fake remedy in history. You
know the one. Don't. It doesn't help, and with some species it makes the sting fire
even more venom. Also skip fresh water — same problem. What actually works: rinse
the area with seawater, gently scrape off any tentacle bits with the edge of a card
— not your bare fingers — then soak the sting in hot water, as warm as you can
comfortably stand, for about 20 minutes. Heat helps break down the venom. And if
you're ever stung on the face, or breathing feels off — that's a medical emergency,
full stop.

---

### #5 — A Kangaroo Squares Up (3:35–4:30)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "5". Australian field. A buff kangaroo flexes. Character
> flexes back — red X. Rewind. He crouches low, coughs into his fist, backs away —
> green check, kangaroo loses interest.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Number 5 — you're in Australia, and a kangaroo is sizing you up. Standing tall to
look intimidating? To a kangaroo, that's accepting the boxing match. Instead, do
the opposite of everything that feels natural: crouch down low, keep your arms in,
back away without turning your back — and here's the strangest expert-approved tip
on this whole list — give a short, low cough. In kangaroo language, that's basically
saying "you win, champ, I'm nobody." Pride slightly dented, face fully intact. Fair
trade.

---

**NARRATOR:**
Quick check-in — which of these animals have YOU actually come face to face with?

### #4 — Bees Swarm You (4:45–5:45)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "4". Angry cloud of bees. Character jumps into a pond — red X;
> bees hover over the surface, tapping tiny wristwatches. Rewind. He sprints for a
> cabin, face covered — green check.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Number 4 — an angry swarm of bees. Cartoon logic says jump into the water. Real
bees say: great, we'll wait. They'll hover right above the surface until you come
up for air. The actual move is the one that feels least heroic — run. Just run, in
a straight line, covering your face with your shirt, and get inside something:
a car, a building, a tent. Bees defend their hive; they don't chase forever. Whatever
you do, don't swat — crushed bees release an alarm scent that tells the others
exactly where the party is.

---

### #3 — A Rip Current Pulls You Out (5:45–6:50)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "3" with spotlight. Top-down beach view; a dark channel of
> water flowing out to sea. Character swims straight at the shore — red X, arrow
> shows zero progress. Rewind. He swims sideways, parallel to the beach — green
> check, escapes the channel.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Number 3 — okay, not an animal, but it's the deadliest thing on this list and it
hides at the same beach as the jellyfish. A rip current: a narrow river of water
flowing away from shore. Your instinct — swim straight back to the beach — puts
you on a treadmill against a current stronger than any Olympic swimmer. The move
that saves lives: don't fight it. Stay calm, float, and swim *parallel* to the
shore — sideways — until you're out of the narrow channel. Then angle back in. And
before you even get in the water: a gap in the waves that looks weirdly calm, or a
line of churning, sandy water heading out? That's the rip. Calm water isn't always
kind water.

---

### #2 — The Bear From the Intro (6:50–8:10)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "2". Back to the cold-open freeze-frame — unfreeze. Character
> runs — red X. Climbs a tree; the bear climbs faster — second red X, bear waves
> from a branch. Rewind. He stands tall, speaks calmly, backs away sideways — green
> check.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
Number 2 — the bear from the beginning, and the one almost everyone gets wrong.
Running triggers a chase you cannot win — a bear can hit 35 miles per hour. Climbing
a tree? Black bears climb better than you do. Here's what experts actually say: stand
your ground. Make yourself look big, speak in a calm, low voice — "hey bear, easy
bear" — so it knows you're a human, not prey, and back away slowly at an angle. No
eye-lock, no sudden moves. If it's a black bear and it actually attacks — fight back,
loud and hard, aiming for the face. If it's a grizzly attacking defensively — the
opposite: lie face down, hands over your neck, legs spread so it can't flip you, and
play dead until it leaves. Two bears, two playbooks — knowing which is which is the
whole game. Most encounters, though, end with the bear leaving first. It wanted the
berries, not you.

---

### #1 — A Mountain Lion Is Watching You (8:10–9:20)
> **[VISUAL:** Number "1". Dusky trail; glowing eyes on a rock. Character crouches
> to grab a stick — red X, the cat leans in. Rewind. He stands huge, jacket spread
> like wings, shouting — green check, the cat backs off.**]**

**NARRATOR:**
And number 1 — the encounter where the rules flip completely: a mountain lion.
Everything we said about being calm and boring? Throw it out. A mountain lion
respects exactly one thing: something too big and too loud to be worth it. Stand
tall, spread your jacket wide, wave your arms, shout in your deepest voice. Never
crouch, never bend down — a crouched shape reads as four-legged prey — and never,
ever turn your back and run. Back away slowly, facing it, looking as huge as
humanly possible. If it attacks, fight with everything — people have driven off
mountain lions with sticks, backpacks, even water bottles. From "be a tree" for the
dog... to "be a giant" for the cat. Same you — completely opposite script. That's
why this list exists.

> **[VISUAL:** Fast montage: all eight icons with their one-word command flashing —

**NARRATOR:**
So there it is — eight encounters, and the eight moves your instincts would've
gotten wrong. Be a tree for the dog, a wall for the bear, a giant for the lion —
and always swim sideways, never straight.

Before you go, one riddle: I have no teeth, no claws, and no venom — yet I'm the
deadliest thing in this video. What am I? First right answer gets a pinned comment.

If this might save your skin someday, pay it forward — like the video, share it
with your hiking buddy, subscribe and ring the bell. Stay safe out there... and
stay on the bright side.

## Production Notes
- **Mistake-first structure:** Every item shows the WRONG move (red X + comedy
  beat) before the right one (green check) — this is the genre's core rhythm and
  its rewatchability engine.
- **Command text:** One shouted-style caption per item ("DON'T RUN!", "SWIM
  SIDEWAYS!") — these become the recap montage, so keep them to 1–3 words.
- **Callback discipline:** The intro bear pays off at #2; "be a tree" (dog, #8)
  inverts at #1 (lion). Callbacks = retention glue.
- **Number bumpers:** Full-screen animated numbers with a claw-swipe transition.
- **Tone guardrail:** Peril stays cartoonish — bears wave, bees wear watches.
  Never show injury; cut away on impact, always resolve to the character safe.
- **Thumbnail:** Split frame — terrified character vs. bear face, red arrow +
  text "DON'T RUN!"; high contrast yellow/red.
- **Music:** Light suspense bed that drops out for each "here's the right move"
  beat — silence sells the tip.
- **Fact hygiene:** All advice mirrors standard guidance (park-service bear
  protocol, lifeguard rip-current guidance, mainstream first-aid for stings).
  Keep language soft ("experts recommend", "nearly all") and add an on-screen
  disclaimer: "For emergencies, always contact local authorities."
