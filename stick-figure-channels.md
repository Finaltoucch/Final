# Stick Figure Channels — Who's Winning, and Where

Search for channels performing well **in our niche** using **our chosen animation
style**.

**Date:** 2026-08-02
**Figures:** third-party aggregators via search. Subscriber counts reliable;
revenue estimates are from marketing blogs and should be discounted heavily.

---

## The Headline Finding

**The style and the niche do not currently overlap.**

Stick figure animation is enormous on YouTube — but it lives almost entirely in
comedy and self-improvement. Business and money explainers are big too, but they
use completely different visual languages.

Nobody of size is doing stick figure business explainers.

That's the whole opportunity, and it's also the honest risk: **there is no proof
it works there, because nobody has done it.**

## The One Channel That Bridges It

### Improvement Pill — 3.77M subscribers

**This is the most important reference on the page.**

It uses clean, instructional stick figure animation to deliver *serious,
non-comedic* content — psychology, habits, discipline, self-improvement. Not
jokes. Not battles. Ideas, explained.

That matters because the obvious objection to our plan is "stick figures are for
comedy, they'll undercut a channel about money." Improvement Pill is 3.77 million
subscribers of evidence against that objection. The style carries instructional
content at scale, and viewers take it seriously.

It's the closest existing proof that the transfer works. It just stops one step
short of our niche — self-improvement pays $5–12, business explainers pay $12–22.

---

## The Thesis, Stated Plainly

**Take the visual style proven at 12.8M subscribers in comedy, and the instructional
credibility proven at 3.77M in self-improvement, into the niche that pays double
either of them.**

Supporting arguments:

1. **Stick figures are the native language of economics.** "Here's you, here's
   the bank, here's where your money goes" is what every economics teacher draws
   on a whiteboard. We're not importing a foreign aesthetic — we're using the
   subject's own visual grammar.
2. **Improvement Pill proves seriousness survives the style.**
3. **The niche's incumbents all look the same as each other** — stock footage,
   basic charts, graded photos. A stick figure channel would be instantly
   distinguishable in the feed.

### The honest counter-arguments

- **No proof.** Nobody has done this at scale, and "nobody has done it" sometimes
  means "it doesn't work." I don't think that's the case here, but it's not
  nothing.
- **Authority risk.** Finance viewers may read stick figures as unserious. Our
  mitigation is tone: rigorous script, sourced numbers, adult narration, restrained
  palette. The visuals can be playful *only if* the substance is not.
- **Saturation is coming.** The AI-stickman tutorial industry is in full swing,
  which means a wave of low-effort imitators within months. Our protection is
  that they'll flood entertainment, where the tutorials point them — and that our
  moat was always the writing.

### An unverified claim, flagged

Tutorial blogs report new AI-stickman channels hitting 3.5M views and 160K subs
in under three months, and one at 20M views from 14 videos. **I could not
identify those channels by name or verify any of it.** The named, verifiable
successes in this style are all long-running human-animated channels. Treat the
AI-stickman gold-rush numbers as marketing until proven otherwise.
